# Course: Technical Writing  (level id "deep-cse-6-8", lesson ids "deep-cse-6-8-01".."-25")
A writing course for CSE students, so every lesson is about one kind of document or one writing skill, taught with before/after rewrites,
small counts (words per sentence, readability scores, budget sums, slide timing) checked with Python, and Bangladesh examples (bKash,
Pathao, lab reports, thesis defence, emailing a teacher). Builds on Software Engineering (deep-cse-4-4: lesson 08 SRS, 25 documentation),
System Analysis & Design (deep-cse-4-6) and HCI (deep-cse-5-5: error messages, labels, Bangla UI). Those are only recapped; here the focus
is the writing itself. Later courses go deeper, so keep these light and point forward: Professional Ethics & Cyber Law (deep-cse-6-6,
copyright and law), Final Year Project / Thesis (deep-cse-6-7), Software Project Management (deep-cse-6-11) and Research Methodology
(deep-cse-7-7). Style guides and standards (RFC 2119, IEEE/APA, Diataxis, Keep a Changelog, OpenAPI, ANSI Z535) appear as version-neutral examples.
01 What technical writing is: definition (help a specific reader understand or do something), technical vs creative vs academic writing, qualities (accurate, clear, concise, complete, usable), document types map, cost of bad writing (Columbia slide, confused users, rejected proposals), how much engineers write, course map
02 Audience and purpose: who reads (expert, technician, manager, layperson), primary/secondary/gatekeeper readers, purpose (inform, instruct, persuade), one-sentence purpose statement, reader context (phone, hurry, second language), audience profile worked, writing for mixed readers (layering)
03 The writing process: prewriting (brainstorm, mind map, freewriting, questions), research and notes, outlining (topic vs sentence outline), drafting fast, revising (big picture), editing (sentences), proofreading (typos), time split worked, writer's block tricks, version history
04 Clear sentences and plain language: plain language idea, sentence length (15-20 average) worked count, one idea per sentence, active vs passive and when passive is right, hidden verbs (nominalizations), strong subjects, positive phrasing, before/after rewrites
05 Precise words: concrete vs vague, numbers with units and SI style, jargon and when it is fine, acronyms on first use, consistent terms, ambiguous pronouns and modifiers, RFC 2119 must/should/may, hedging honestly, words to cut
06 Paragraphs, flow and lists: topic sentence, one idea per paragraph, old-to-new flow, transitions, parallel structure, bulleted vs numbered lists and when not to list, BLUF and inverted pyramid, worked paragraph rebuild
07 Grammar and punctuation for engineers: subject-verb agreement, articles a/an/the, tense in reports, comma splice and run-ons, apostrophes (its/it's), dangling modifiers, colon and semicolon, capitalisation, common Bangladeshi English slips (discuss about, return back, cope up, revert), checking tools
08 Conciseness and readability: wordy phrases table, redundancy pairs, cutting worked (word count before/after), Flesch Reading Ease, Flesch-Kincaid grade and Gunning Fog worked, what the scores miss, readability for Bangla readers, when longer is better
09 Document design and layout: how people scan (F-pattern idea), headings hierarchy and numbering, white space, typefaces and size, line length 45-75 characters, alignment, emphasis rules, templates and styles, accessibility (contrast 4.5:1, alt text, real headings)
10 Figures and tables: when to use text vs table vs chart vs diagram, chart choice (bar, line, scatter, pie limits), labels and units, captions (IEEE: table above, figure below), referring in text, truncated axis worked, honest visuals, diagrams and screenshots
11 Emails and workplace messages: subject lines, BLUF, one clear ask, tone and politeness, emailing a teacher worked, cc/bcc/reply-all, follow-ups, chat messages, asking a good technical question (bug report style), meeting notes and action items
12 Definitions and descriptions: parenthetical, sentence (term + class + features) and extended definitions, operational definitions, mechanism description (overall, parts, how they work together), process description, worked examples (router, OTP login)
13 Instructions and procedures: when instructions are needed, structure (title, purpose, prerequisites, steps, result, troubleshooting), imperative mood, one action per step, numbering and sub-steps, safety notices (DANGER, WARNING, CAUTION, NOTICE), screenshots, testing instructions with a real user
14 User manuals and help: kinds of user docs, Diataxis (tutorial, how-to, reference, explanation), task-based topics, minimalism (Carroll), getting started guides, FAQs, troubleshooting tables, error messages recap (deep-cse-5-5), release notes, Bangla localisation
15 README files and code comments: what a README is for, sections (what, install, usage, config, contribute, licence), Markdown basics, good vs bad comments (why not what), docstrings, naming as documentation, commit messages, docs-as-code
16 API and design documentation: API reference (endpoint, parameters, request, response, errors, auth), examples first, OpenAPI idea, versioning and semantic versioning, changelogs (Keep a Changelog), design docs and ADRs, keeping docs fresh
17 Requirements and specifications language: recap SRS (deep-cse-4-4-08), good requirement qualities (clear, testable, atomic, feasible), shall statements, EARS patterns, user stories and acceptance criteria wording, ambiguity hunt worked, glossary
18 Technical reports, kinds and structure: report types (lab, progress, feasibility, recommendation, incident/postmortem, research), front matter / body / back matter, IMRaD, executive summary, numbering, appendices, progress report and blameless postmortem worked
19 Writing the report sections: title, abstract (informative vs descriptive, 150-250 words), introduction (problem, gap, aim), methods (enough to repeat), results vs discussion, conclusion and recommendations, worked abstract for a lab experiment
20 Proposals: solicited vs unsolicited, internal vs external, problem, objectives (SMART), method, timeline (Gantt), budget worked, evaluation plan, persuasion with evidence, thesis/project proposal shape (point to deep-cse-6-7)
21 Research papers and thesis: paper anatomy, contributions list, related work and literature review, CARS model for introductions, thesis chapters, conference vs journal, peer review and responding to reviewers, point to deep-cse-7-7
22 Citing and referencing: why cite, what needs citing, quote vs paraphrase vs summary, IEEE style worked, APA style worked, in-text citations, reference lists, reference managers and BibTeX, citing web pages, software and datasets
23 Plagiarism, integrity and AI tools: forms of plagiarism (copy-paste, mosaic, self, idea, accidental), how similarity checkers work and what a percentage means, avoiding plagiarism, AI writing tools honestly used and disclosed, copyright and licences (Creative Commons) brief (point to deep-cse-6-6), honest data and figures
24 Presentations and posters: talk vs document, know the room, structure (story arc), assertion-evidence slides, text on slides, timing worked, delivery and practice, demos, Q&A, thesis defence, posters
25 Editing, reviewing and capstone: levels of edit (substantive, copyedit, proofread), giving and receiving feedback, checklists, style guides, tools, collaborative writing and version control for docs, capstone: a full doc set for an invented "BusKoi" bus-tracking app, course recap and what next
