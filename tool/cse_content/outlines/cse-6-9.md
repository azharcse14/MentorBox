# Course: VLSI Design  (level id "deep-cse-6-9", lesson ids "deep-cse-6-9-01".."-25")
Builds on Electronic Devices & Circuits (deep-cse-2-4: lessons 18-20 MOSFET regions, CMOS inverter/NAND/NOR, lesson 25 fabrication basics),
Digital Logic Design (deep-cse-2-3: gates, Boolean algebra, adders, flip-flops, FSMs, lesson 24 PLA/FPGA intro, lesson 25 Verilog basics)
and Computer Architecture (deep-cse-3-2: ALU, adders, multipliers, pipelining, SRAM/DRAM, caches). Those are recapped in a line or two;
here the focus is how a chip is actually designed: transistor-level circuits, layout, delay, power, interconnect, timing, datapath and
memory arrays, HDL for synthesis, the ASIC and FPGA flows, and test. Weste & Harris / Rabaey style, with small worked numbers
(currents, RC and Elmore delay, logical effort, power, timing slack, MTBF, wafer cost, fault coverage) checked with Python.
Technology numbers are illustrative and labelled so. Later courses go deeper: Parallel & HPC (deep-cse-7-5), Fault Tolerant Systems
(deep-cse-7-10), Quantum Computing (deep-cse-9-3), IoT (deep-cse-9-1); Embedded Systems (deep-cse-5-6) is where FPGAs/MCUs meet products.
01 What VLSI is: integration scales (SSI, MSI, LSI, VLSI), Moore's law (1965/1975) with 4004 to modern SoC counts worked, why integrate (cost, speed, power, reliability), chip types (full-custom, standard-cell ASIC, gate array, FPGA), abstraction levels and the Gajski Y-chart, industry (fabless, foundry, IDM, EDA), Bangladesh chip-design companies, course map
02 The MOS transistor as a switch: recap structure (gate, oxide, source, drain, body), nMOS vs pMOS, threshold voltage, switch-level model, strong 0 / weak 1 (VDD - Vt worked), why pMOS pulls up and nMOS pulls down, transmission gate preview, CMOS = complementary pair, symbols and terminals
03 MOSFET I-V characteristics: cutoff, linear (triode), saturation, long-channel equations worked (72 uA and 40 uA), k' and W/L, body effect worked, channel-length modulation, velocity saturation and short-channel effects, subthreshold leakage and the 60-100 mV/decade slope, simple RC switch model preview
04 The CMOS inverter in depth: VTC and its five regions, switching threshold VM formula worked (0.5 V vs 0.466 V), beta ratio and sizing pMOS 2-3x, noise margins, static power near zero, regenerative property, inverter as the yardstick for every other gate
05 Static CMOS logic gates: pull-up and pull-down networks, series/parallel duality, NAND/NOR recap, building any complementary gate from an expression step by step, AOI/OAI compound gates (AOI22: 8 vs 16 transistors), transistor sizing for equal drive, why NAND beats NOR, mirror full adder (28T), limits on fan-in
06 Other logic styles: pass-transistor logic and its threshold drop, transmission gates (MUX and XOR), ratioed pseudo-nMOS logic and static power, dynamic logic (precharge/evaluate), domino logic and the monotonicity rule, charge sharing worked (0.67 V), keepers, CVSL brief, choosing a style
07 How chips are made: silicon to wafer, cleanroom, oxidation, photolithography (masks, resist, exposure, DUV/EUV), etching, ion implantation, deposition, CMP, n-well CMOS process step by step, front-end vs back-end (metal stack), process nodes and naming, FinFET/GAA brief, dies per wafer and yield worked (640 dies, 90.5 percent, about 17 dollars per good die)
08 Layout and design rules: layers (well, active, poly, contact, metal, via), lambda vs micron rules (width, spacing, enclosure), why rules exist, stick diagrams, Euler paths for single-strip gate layout, standard-cell template (rails, fixed height), DRC, extraction, LVS, a full inverter and NAND2 layout walk-through
09 Resistance and capacitance: sheet resistance and squares worked (800 ohm wire), contact/via resistance, gate, diffusion and wire capacitance, effective transistor resistance (R for unit nMOS, 2R for pMOS of same width), RC models of gates, Elmore delay worked (70 ps), lumped vs distributed RC (0.5RC vs 0.38RC)
10 Delay of gates: propagation delay, rise/fall/contamination delay, RC delay of an inverter driving a load, tau, parasitic delay, fanout and FO4 delay, effect of sizing, stacked transistors, input ordering, delay vs VDD and temperature
11 Logical effort: g, h, p, d = gh + p, logical effort table (inv 1, NAND2 4/3, NOR2 5/3), path effort G, B, H, F, best stage effort F^(1/N), optimum stage count about log4 F, worked path sizing (F 85.3, f 4.4, delay 17.2 tau, sizes 1, 4.4, 14.5), buffering a large load, limitations
12 Power and energy: where power goes (dynamic, short-circuit, leakage), P = alpha C V^2 f worked (0.2 W), activity factor, energy per operation, power-delay and energy-delay product, leakage types (subthreshold, gate, junction), leakage vs temperature, power density and thermal limits, dark silicon idea
13 Low-power design: why phones care, voltage scaling (V^2), DVFS worked (0.48x), clock gating, operand isolation, power gating with sleep transistors and retention, multi-Vt cells (10x leakage per decade), body biasing, multiple voltage islands and level shifters, near-threshold, architecture tricks (parallelism at lower V)
14 Interconnect: wire scaling, metal stack and pitch, wire RC grows with L^2, repeaters worked (760 ps vs about 250 ps), crosstalk and coupling capacitance, shielding, IR drop worked (50 mV), electromigration, copper and low-k, power grid design, the wire as the bottleneck
15 Scaling and its limits: Dennard constant-field scaling table, what scaled and what didn't, end of Dennard scaling (about 2005), leakage and variability, short-channel effects, high-k metal gate, strained silicon, FinFET and gate-all-around, Moore's law economics, More-than-Moore, chiplets preview
16 Sequential circuits at transistor level: static vs dynamic storage, latch (transmission-gate and clocked CMOS), master-slave flip-flop, timing parameters (tpcq, tccq, setup, hold), max-delay constraint worked (2.08 GHz, 2 GHz with skew), min-delay/hold constraint worked, clock skew and jitter, pulsed latches brief
17 Clocking, timing analysis and synchronization: static timing analysis (arrival, required, slack worked), critical path, multi-corner analysis, clock distribution (H-tree, grid, CTS), PLL brief, metastability, synchronizers and MTBF worked (2.2 million years vs 1e28), clock domain crossing, retiming
18 Datapath subsystems: ripple-carry adder delay (32 x 20 ps), carry-lookahead, carry-select, carry-skip, prefix adders (Kogge-Stone, log2 N levels), comparators, barrel shifter (5 stages, 160 muxes), array multiplier, Booth radix-4, Wallace tree (8 -> 6 -> 4 -> 3 -> 2), area-delay-power trade-offs
19 Memory arrays: why memory dominates chip area, array organisation (rows, columns, decoders, sense amplifiers), 6T SRAM cell read and write, read stability and cell sizing, 1 MB SRAM = about 50 million transistors, DRAM 1T1C and refresh, ROM, flash (floating gate), CAM brief, redundancy and ECC
20 Verilog for hardware I: HDL vs software, module and ports, wire vs reg, continuous assign, always @(*) combinational, always @(posedge clk) sequential, blocking vs non-blocking worked swap example, vectors and operators, structural vs behavioural, a 4-bit adder and its testbench
21 Verilog for hardware II: finite state machines (Moore traffic light), parameters and generate, synthesizable vs simulation-only code, latch inference pitfalls, reset styles, counters and shift registers, simulation with delays, VHDL and SystemVerilog brief, coding guidelines
22 The ASIC design flow: specification and architecture, RTL, functional verification (testbench, coverage, assertions), logic synthesis (libraries, constraints, SDC), DFT insertion, floorplanning, placement, clock-tree synthesis, routing, STA sign-off, DRC/LVS, power and IR analysis, tape-out, PDK and EDA tools, open-source flow (OpenROAD, SkyWater 130) brief
23 FPGAs: inside an FPGA (LUTs, flip-flops, CLBs, routing, block RAM, DSP slices, IO, SRAM configuration), a 6-LUT holds 64 bits, FPGA flow (synthesis, map, place and route, bitstream), timing on FPGAs, FPGA vs ASIC (speed, power, cost) with break-even worked (about 41,667 units), prototyping and acceleration uses
24 Testing and design for test: defects vs faults, stuck-at model, test vectors and fault coverage worked (98 percent), why exhaustive testing fails (2^64 vectors = 584 years), controllability and observability, scan chains, ATPG, BIST with LFSR trace, JTAG boundary scan, yield, defect level worked (about 2100 DPPM)
25 Capstone and wrap-up: designing a small 8-bit ALU chip end to end (spec, RTL, verify, synthesize, timing, power, layout, test), SoC and IP reuse, packaging brief, analog and mixed-signal brief, careers and Bangladesh industry, course recap, what next (deep-cse-7-5, deep-cse-7-10, deep-cse-9-1, deep-cse-9-3)
