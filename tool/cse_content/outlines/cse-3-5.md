# Course: Theory of Computation  (level id "deep-cse-3-5", lesson ids "deep-cse-3-5-01".."-25")
Builds on Discrete Mathematics (deep-cse-1-3: sets, functions, relations, proof by induction and contradiction, graphs) and
Data Structures (deep-cse-2-2: stack). Those are only recapped; here the focus is pretend machines, what each one can and
cannot recognise, and the hard limits of computing. Machines are always traced on real input strings, step by step.
01 What computation is: problem as a yes/no question, why we use pretend machines (models) instead of real laptops, history (Hilbert's question, Turing 1936, Chomsky 1956), the three big questions (automata, computability, complexity), course map
02 Alphabets, strings and languages: alphabet Σ, string, length, empty string ε, concatenation, reversal, powers, Σ*, Σ+, prefix/suffix/substring, language as a set of strings, finite vs infinite languages, operations on languages (union, concatenation, Kleene star), decision problem = membership in a language
03 Proof tools for this course: set notation recap, proof by construction, contradiction, induction on string length, pigeonhole principle (the heart of pumping lemmas), countable vs uncountable sets and diagonalization idea (why some languages have no machine at all)
04 Deterministic finite automata (DFA): states, start, accept states, transition function, 5-tuple, state diagram and transition table, trace on strings, designing DFAs (even number of 1s, ends with 01, divisible by 3 in binary), dead/trap state
05 Designing DFAs well: "what must I remember?" method, product construction (AND/OR of two DFAs), complement by swapping accept states, extended transition function, real uses (vending machine, lexer, turnstile), limits (finite memory)
06 Nondeterministic finite automata (NFA): multiple/zero moves, ε-moves, "guessing" view vs "parallel copies" view, 5-tuple, tracing sets of states, ε-closure, why NFAs are easier to design (third-last symbol is 1)
07 NFA to DFA: subset construction step by step, worked example with ε-moves, unreachable subsets, exponential blow-up (2^n), equivalence of DFA and NFA power
08 DFA minimization: equivalent and distinguishable states, unreachable state removal, table-filling (Myhill-Nerode) algorithm worked, partition refinement, uniqueness of minimal DFA, Myhill-Nerode idea
09 Regular expressions: syntax (union, concatenation, star, precedence), building regexes for given languages, identities (ε, ∅, (a+b)*), real regex in programming vs theory regex, grep/email/phone examples
10 Regex and automata equivalence: Thompson's construction (regex to ε-NFA) worked, state elimination (DFA to regex) worked, Arden's theorem, Kleene's theorem
11 Pumping lemma for regular languages: intuition from pigeonhole (long string must loop), formal statement, proof game (adversary), proving a^n b^n, palindromes, a^(n^2) non-regular, common mistakes (using it to prove regularity)
12 Closure and decision properties of regular languages: union, intersection, complement, concatenation, star, reversal, difference, homomorphism; using closure to prove non-regularity; decision problems (emptiness, finiteness, equivalence, membership); Moore and Mealy machines (FA with output)
13 Context-free grammars: why regex is not enough (nesting), variables, terminals, productions, start symbol, 4-tuple, derivation, grammars for a^n b^n, palindromes, balanced brackets, arithmetic expressions, BNF in real languages
14 Derivations, parse trees and ambiguity: leftmost/rightmost derivation, parse tree, yield, ambiguous grammar (E -> E+E | E*E | id), removing ambiguity with precedence levels, inherently ambiguous languages, dangling else
15 Simplifying grammars and normal forms: useless symbols, ε-productions, unit productions removal worked, Chomsky Normal Form conversion worked, 2n-1 steps, Greibach Normal Form idea
16 Pushdown automata: finite control + stack, transitions (read, pop, push), 7-tuple, instantaneous description, PDA for a^n b^n traced, for ww^R (nondeterminism), acceptance by final state vs empty stack
17 CFG and PDA equivalence, DPDA: CFG to PDA construction worked, PDA to CFG idea, deterministic PDA weaker than NPDA, DCFLs and why compilers use them (LL/LR parsing idea), CYK membership algorithm worked
18 Pumping lemma for CFLs and closure: parse tree height argument, uvxyz statement, proving a^n b^n c^n not context-free, closure under union/concatenation/star, not under intersection/complement, intersection with regular
19 Turing machines: Turing's idea (human computer with paper), tape, head, states, transition function, 7-tuple, configuration, TM for a^n b^n c^n traced, TM computing a function (unary addition), accept/reject/loop
20 Turing machine variants and power: multi-tape, nondeterministic, two-way infinite, enumerators, simulation arguments (why all equivalent), universal Turing machine, encoding a machine as a string, stored-program computer link
21 Church-Turing thesis and decidability: algorithm = TM that always halts, decidable vs recognizable (semi-decidable) vs co-recognizable, decidable problems about DFAs/CFGs (A_DFA, E_DFA, A_CFG), why "thesis" not "theorem"
22 The halting problem: statement, proof by contradiction with diagonalization (program D) step by step, A_TM undecidable, complement not recognizable, real consequences (no perfect bug/virus/infinite loop detector)
23 Reductions and Rice's theorem: what a reduction is (use solving B to solve A), mapping reductions, HALT_TM, E_TM, REGULAR_TM undecidable, Post Correspondence Problem example, Rice's theorem statement and use, what it does NOT say
24 Chomsky hierarchy: type 0-3 grammars, matching machines (TM, LBA, PDA, FA), context-sensitive languages and LBA, containment picture, comparison table of power/memory/closure/decision
25 Complexity: P, NP and NP-completeness + wrap-up: time complexity on TMs, class P, verifier view of NP, examples (SAT, Hamiltonian path, subset sum), polynomial reductions, Cook-Levin, NP-complete and NP-hard, P vs NP open problem, coping (approximation, heuristics), course recap and what next
