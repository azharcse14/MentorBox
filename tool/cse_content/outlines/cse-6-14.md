# Course: Data Science  (level id "deep-cse-6-14", lesson ids "deep-cse-6-14-01".."-25")
Builds on Probability & Statistics (deep-cse-2-6: lessons 02-04 charts and summary statistics, 09 Bayes, 17-18 CLT and sampling, 20-22
confidence intervals and tests, 24 correlation and regression), Database Management Systems (deep-cse-4-2: relational model and SQL) and
Machine Learning (deep-cse-6-1: lessons 02-04 workflow, features and splitting, 05-19 the models, 12-14 evaluation, CV and imbalance,
21-23 clustering and PCA, 24-25 toolkit and fairness). Those are only recapped; here the focus is the whole data science workflow done well
in practice: asking the question, getting and cleaning data, exploring and visualizing it, statistics for real decisions, features, modeling
as a practitioner, communicating, and doing it ethically. Later courses go deeper and are only pointed to: Data Mining (deep-cse-7-6),
Advanced Database Systems & Big Data (deep-cse-7-2), Data Engineering (deep-cse-7-11), Deep Learning (deep-cse-8-1), NLP (deep-cse-8-2),
Professional Ethics & Cyber Law (deep-cse-6-6). Tools (Python, Jupyter, NumPy, pandas, SQL, matplotlib, seaborn, scikit-learn, BI tools)
are described as concepts with small version-neutral snippets; worked numbers checked with Python.
01 What data science is: the question-to-decision journey, Drew Conway's Venn diagram (hacking, math and statistics, domain knowledge), data science vs statistics vs machine learning vs data mining vs data engineering vs business intelligence, roles (analyst, scientist, engineer, ML engineer), descriptive/diagnostic/predictive/prescriptive analytics, Bangladesh examples, course map
02 The data science life cycle: CRISP-DM's six phases (business understanding, data understanding, data preparation, modeling, evaluation, deployment), OSEMN, why it loops, where time really goes, a pharmacy stock-out project walked through every phase, common ways projects fail
03 Asking good questions and choosing metrics: vague wish to sharp question, unit of analysis, target and population, metrics vs KPIs, leading vs lagging, proxy metrics and Goodhart's law, north star and guardrail metrics, success criteria before analysis, Fermi estimation worked, writing hypotheses up front
04 Data types, tidy data and file formats: structured, semi-structured, unstructured, variable types (numeric, nominal, ordinal, datetime, text, boolean), tidy data's three rules, wide vs long worked, CSV, JSON, Excel, Parquet and columnar idea, UTF-8 and Bangla text, data dictionary and metadata
05 Collecting data: primary vs secondary, surveys and question wording, sampling recap and sampling bias (selection, non-response, survivorship, 1936 Literary Digest poll), experiments, logs and instrumentation, sensors, open data portals, APIs (requests, JSON, pagination, rate limits, keys), web scraping and robots.txt, legal and ethical limits, sample size thinking
06 SQL for data science: relational recap, SELECT/WHERE/ORDER BY/LIMIT, aggregates with GROUP BY and HAVING worked on a small sales table, logical query order, INNER vs LEFT JOIN worked, join fan-out double counting, NULL traps, CTEs, window functions (running total, RANK, LAG) worked, push work to the database
07 Python, notebooks and NumPy: why Python, Jupyter cells and kernel, hidden state and out-of-order execution, NumPy arrays vs lists, vectorization and speed, broadcasting worked, dtypes and NaN, random seeds, environments and requirements, R, Excel and others compared, notebook vs script
08 pandas fundamentals: Series and DataFrame, index, read_csv options, head/info/describe, selecting with [], loc and iloc, boolean masks, new columns, sorting, value_counts, dtypes and conversion, string and date accessors, apply vs vectorized, method chaining, copy vs view warning, worked on a small shop table
09 Grouping, joining and reshaping in pandas: split-apply-combine, groupby with agg worked, several aggregates, transform for group-wise columns, merge with join types and row-count checks, validate, concat, pivot_table worked, melt from wide to long, time resampling
10 Data cleaning I, types, formats and duplicates: data quality dimensions, inspecting first, fixing types, trimming and case, mapping messy categories (Dhaka/dhaka/Dhk), parsing prices like "5,000 tk", dates in mixed formats, units, exact and near duplicates, validation rules and range checks, keeping raw data and a cleaning log
11 Data cleaning II, missing values and outliers: how missing values look, MCAR/MAR/MNAR, counting missingness, dropping vs imputing, mean/median/mode worked, group-wise imputation, missing indicator, forward fill, outliers by IQR fence worked and z-score worked, error vs real extreme, capping and log transform, documenting decisions
12 Exploratory data analysis I, one variable at a time: EDA mindset (Tukey), summary statistics recap, shape (symmetric, skewed, bimodal), histogram and bin width, box plot, KDE idea, mean vs median under skew worked, log transform worked, categorical counts, rare categories, Anscombe's quartet
13 Exploratory data analysis II, relationships: scatter plots, correlation matrix, Pearson vs Spearman recap, cross-tabs and row percentages, comparing groups, Simpson's paradox worked with numbers, time series EDA (trend, seasonality, moving average worked), pair plots, EDA checklist
14 Data visualization principles: chart choice by question, Cleveland and McGill's ranking of encodings, bar vs pie, histogram vs bar, line charts, truncated axes and lie factor worked, dual axes, 3D and chartjunk, data-ink, colour (sequential, diverging, categorical, colour-blind safe), labels and titles that state the finding, small multiples
15 Visualization tools and dashboards: matplotlib figure and axes, seaborn, plotly interactivity, BI tools (Power BI, Tableau, Looker Studio) as concepts, maps and choropleths, dashboard design (KPI tiles on top, trends, filters, refresh), vanity vs actionable metrics, a delivery company dashboard designed, performance and data freshness
16 Statistics for decisions I, estimation and uncertainty: population vs sample recap, standard error worked, confidence interval for a mean and a proportion worked, reading an interval correctly, bootstrap idea worked by hand, margin of error and sample size worked, practical vs statistical significance
17 Statistics for decisions II, A/B testing: experiment design (unit, randomization, metric, guardrails), two-proportion z test worked, p-value meaning and common misreadings, sample size and power worked, peeking problem, multiple testing and Bonferroni worked, novelty effect, sample ratio mismatch, when not to A/B test
18 Correlation, causation and causal thinking: confounding, reverse causation, selection bias, spurious correlations, randomized experiments, natural experiments, difference-in-differences worked, regression with controls idea, causal diagrams (DAG) idea, Bradford Hill style questions
19 Feature engineering in practice: recap of deep-cse-6-1 lesson 03, date features (weekday, month, Eid/holiday flags), aggregations and RFM worked, ratios and differences, binning, log transform, encoding high-cardinality categories (frequency, target encoding and leakage), text basics (counts, TF-IDF idea), interactions, feature selection, leakage through features
20 Modeling in practice I, baselines and the loop: framing (regression, classification, forecasting, clustering, ranking), baselines worked (mean, majority, last value), simple model first, preprocessing in a pipeline fit on train only, cross-validation recap, metric that matches business cost with a cost matrix worked, iterate on data not just models
21 Modeling in practice II, interpreting, forecasting and checking: reading linear coefficients, feature importance and permutation importance worked, partial dependence idea, SHAP named, error analysis by segment, calibration worked, time series forecasting (naive, seasonal naive, moving average, time-based split, MAE/MAPE worked), model card, monitoring and drift recap
22 Unsupervised tools for analysts: customer segmentation (RFM scores plus k-means recap), naming and checking segments, PCA for plotting recap, anomaly detection (z-score, IQR, isolation forest idea), market basket analysis with support, confidence and lift worked, points to deep-cse-7-6
23 Communicating results and data storytelling: know the audience, answer first (pyramid principle), one chart one message, from finding to recommendation, stating uncertainty in plain words, executive summary worked, slides vs notebooks vs dashboards, reproducible reports, handling pushback and "so what?"
24 Ethics, privacy and responsible data science: consent and purpose, personal and sensitive data, anonymization and re-identification (Sweeney's ZIP, birth date and sex finding), k-anonymity worked, differential privacy idea, bias sources and a fairness check worked, p-hacking and honest reporting, data protection laws (GDPR as example; point to deep-cse-6-6), checklist
25 Reproducible workflow and capstone: project folder structure, git for code, data versioning idea, environments, pipelines (point to deep-cse-7-11, deep-cse-7-2), capstone for an invented Dhaka online grocery "BazarKoro" with late deliveries (question, SQL pull, cleaning, EDA, test, model, recommendation, ethics), course recap and what next
