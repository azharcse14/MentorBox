# Course: Data Communication  (level id "deep-cse-3-6", lesson ids "deep-cse-3-6-01".."-25")
Builds on Electrical Circuits (deep-cse-1-5), Complex Variables, Fourier & Laplace (deep-cse-2-7) and Probability (deep-cse-2-6).
Those are only recapped. Signals & Systems (cse-3-7) goes deeper into transforms; Computer Networks (cse-4-3) covers IP, routing,
TCP/UDP and applications. Here the focus is how bits physically travel: signals, encoding, modulation, media, multiplexing,
switching, error control and the data link layer. Always worked with small numbers (bit rates, dB, Nyquist/Shannon, CRC by hand).
01 What data communication is: five components (message, sender, receiver, medium, protocol), data representation (text, numbers, images, audio, video), data flow (simplex, half-duplex, full-duplex), network criteria (performance, reliability, security), course map
02 Networks and topologies: point-to-point vs multipoint links, physical topologies (mesh with n(n-1)/2 links, star, bus, ring, hybrid), LAN/MAN/WAN, internet vs Internet, standards bodies (ISO, ITU-T, IEEE, IETF), protocol elements (syntax, semantics, timing)
03 Protocol layering: why layers (post office analogy), OSI 7 layers, TCP/IP 5-layer suite, encapsulation and headers, addresses at each layer (MAC, IP, port), where this course sits (physical + data link)
04 Data and signals: analog vs digital data and signals, periodic vs non-periodic, sine wave (peak amplitude, frequency, period f = 1/T, phase in degrees), wavelength = speed / frequency, worked examples
05 Time domain, frequency domain and bandwidth: same signal two views, composite signals, Fourier idea (square wave = sum of sines), bandwidth = fmax - fmin, digital signals (bit rate, bit length, levels L and log2 L bits), baseband vs broadband (modulated) transmission
06 Transmission impairments: attenuation and decibels (dB = 10 log10 P2/P1), amplifiers and repeaters, distortion (different speeds of components), noise (thermal, induced, crosstalk, impulse), signal-to-noise ratio and SNR in dB
07 Data rate limits and performance: Nyquist bit rate 2B log2 L for noiseless channel, Shannon capacity B log2(1+SNR), using both together, bandwidth in Hz vs bits/s, throughput, latency (propagation, transmission, queuing, processing), bandwidth-delay product, jitter
08 Line coding: digital to digital, signal element vs data element, DC component, baseline wandering, self-synchronization, unipolar NRZ, polar NRZ-L and NRZ-I, RZ, Manchester and differential Manchester, bipolar AMI and pseudoternary, multilevel idea (2B1Q), drawing waveforms for 01001110
09 Block coding and scrambling: why line coding alone is not enough, 4B/5B (Fast Ethernet with NRZ-I), 8B/10B, error detection side benefit, scrambling with B8ZS (North America) and HDB3 (Europe/Bangladesh E1) worked
10 Analog to digital conversion: pulse code modulation steps (sampling, quantizing, encoding), Nyquist sampling theorem fs >= 2 fmax, quantization levels and error, SQNR about 6.02 nb + 1.76 dB, telephone voice 8000 x 8 = 64 kbps, delta modulation and its slope overload
11 Transmission modes: parallel vs serial (cost, skew), asynchronous serial (start bit, stop bit, efficiency worked), synchronous serial (frames, no gaps), isochronous; UART, USB, keyboard examples
12 Digital to analog: modulation of a carrier, bit rate vs baud rate (N = S x r), ASK, BFSK, BPSK, QPSK, constellation diagrams, QAM (16-QAM 4 bits per symbol), bandwidth of ASK/PSK (1+d)S, why phone/WiFi use QAM
13 Analog to analog: why we modulate audio (antenna size, sharing), AM (bandwidth 2B, 530-1700 kHz band), FM (bandwidth about 2(1+beta)B, 88-108 MHz, 200 kHz channel), PM, noise behaviour; Bangladesh FM radio and Bangladesh Betar examples
14 Multiplexing part 1, FDM and WDM: why share a link, multiplexer/demultiplexer, frequency-division with guard bands worked, analog telephone hierarchy (group 12 channels, supergroup 60), wavelength-division in fiber, prism analogy
15 Multiplexing part 2, TDM: synchronous TDM (time slots, frames, interleaving, framing bits), data rate worked, empty slots, statistical TDM (addresses in slots), digital hierarchy T1 1.544 Mbps (24 x 64 kbps + 8 kbps), E1 2.048 Mbps (32 x 64 kbps)
16 Spread spectrum: why spread (anti-jamming, privacy, sharing), FHSS (pseudorandom hopping, Bluetooth 79 channels 1600 hops/s), DSSS (chips, 11-chip Barker code in original 802.11), spreading factor, comparison
17 Guided media: twisted pair (why twisting cancels noise, UTP vs STP, categories Cat5e/Cat6, RJ45), coaxial cable (structure, cable TV), fiber optics (core, cladding, total internal reflection, multimode step/graded, single-mode, advantages, submarine cable SEA-ME-WE)
18 Unguided media: electromagnetic spectrum, propagation (ground, sky, line-of-sight), radio waves, microwaves (terrestrial towers, satellite, GEO about 35,786 km), infrared, antennas (omnidirectional vs directional), Bangabandhu Satellite-1 example
19 Switching: why switch instead of mesh, circuit switching (setup, data, teardown, phone call), packet switching (datagram vs virtual circuit, tables), message switching (store and forward), delay comparison worked
20 Using telephone and cable networks: PSTN structure (local loop, end offices), dial-up modems (V.90 56 kbps), DSL (ADSL asymmetric, splitter, DMT idea), cable TV networks and HFC, cable modem, from copper to fiber (FTTH)
21 Error detection basics: single-bit vs burst errors, redundancy, detection vs correction, block coding view (datawords, codewords), Hamming distance and dmin rules (detect s errors needs dmin >= s+1, correct t needs 2t+1), simple parity, 2D parity worked
22 CRC and checksum: cyclic codes, polynomial notation, modulo-2 division worked step by step, generator choice, what CRC catches, Internet checksum with one's complement worked, CRC-32 in Ethernet
23 Error correction: forward error correction vs retransmission, Hamming(7,4) code built and syndrome worked, which bit flipped, code rate, interleaving for burst errors, real FEC in phones, CDs and satellite
24 Data link control: framing (character-oriented with byte stuffing, bit-oriented with flag 01111110 and bit stuffing), flow vs error control, Stop-and-Wait ARQ, sliding window, Go-Back-N, Selective Repeat (window size limits), piggybacking, HDLC and PPP brief
25 Media access control and wrap-up: random access (pure ALOHA 18.4%, slotted ALOHA 36.8%), CSMA (1-persistent, non-persistent, p-persistent), CSMA/CD (Ethernet), CSMA/CA (WiFi), controlled access (polling, token passing), channelization (FDMA, TDMA, CDMA with Walsh codes), course recap and what next
