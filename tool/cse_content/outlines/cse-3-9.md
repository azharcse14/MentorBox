# Course: Graph Theory  (level id "deep-cse-3-9", lesson ids "deep-cse-3-9-01".."-25")
Builds on Discrete Mathematics (deep-cse-1-3, lessons 23-25: graph words, handshaking, Euler/Hamilton, planar, colouring, trees named), Data Structures (deep-cse-2-2: adjacency matrix/list, BFS, DFS, topological sort as code) and Algorithms (deep-cse-3-1: Kruskal/Prim, Dijkstra/Bellman-Ford, Ford-Fulkerson/Edmonds-Karp in depth with complexity). Those are recapped in a few lines only. Here the focus is graph theory proper: definitions, theorems, proofs explained simply, and why they are true.
Left for other courses: algorithm complexity proofs and union-find details stay in Algorithms (deep-cse-3-1); NP-completeness theory goes to Theory of Computation (deep-cse-3-5) and Advanced Algorithms (deep-cse-7-1); routing protocols go to Computer Networks (deep-cse-4-3); graph neural networks go to Deep Learning. Mention and move on.
01 What a graph is: vertices and edges as dots and lines, Konigsberg story, G = (V, E), order and size, simple graphs vs multigraphs vs pseudographs (loops), directed vs undirected, weighted, finite, where graphs show up, course map
02 Degree and the handshake lemma: degree, neighbourhood, isolated and pendant vertices, sum of degrees = 2|E|, number of odd-degree vertices is even, degree sequence, Havel-Hakimi test for graphic sequences, in-degree and out-degree for digraphs
03 Special families of graphs: null graph, complete graph Kn (n(n-1)/2 edges), cycle Cn, path Pn, wheel Wn, hypercube Qn (2^n vertices, n 2^(n-1) edges), regular graphs, complete bipartite Km,n, Petersen graph
04 Representing graphs: adjacency matrix (symmetric, row sum = degree), adjacency list, incidence matrix (column sums = 2), edge list, space trade-offs, powers of the adjacency matrix count walks (A^k entry = walks of length k)
05 Graph isomorphism: same structure different drawing, bijection preserving edges, invariants (order, size, degree sequence, cycles, connectedness), proving two graphs are NOT isomorphic, worked examples, why the general problem is hard (no known fast method)
06 Subgraphs, complements and operations: subgraph, spanning subgraph, induced subgraph, complement (edges of Kn minus G), self-complementary graphs, union, intersection, deleting vertices/edges, contraction, line graph idea
07 Walks, trails, paths and cycles: definitions and the differences, length, closed vs open, distance d(u,v), eccentricity, diameter, radius, centre, girth, every u-v walk contains a u-v path
08 Connectivity: connected graphs and components, at least n - k edges with k components, a connected graph has at least n - 1 edges, strongly vs weakly connected digraphs, condensation idea
09 Cut vertices, bridges and blocks: cut vertex, bridge (an edge is a bridge iff it is on no cycle), vertex connectivity κ, edge connectivity λ, Whitney κ ≤ λ ≤ δ, Menger's theorem (statement), 2-connected graphs, finding them with DFS (low values idea)
10 Euler trails and circuits: Konigsberg solved, Euler's theorem (connected + all even degrees iff Euler circuit; exactly 0 or 2 odd vertices for a trail), proof idea, Fleury's and Hierholzer's methods, directed version, Chinese postman idea
11 Hamilton paths and cycles: definition, examples (dodecahedron game, Kn), no simple test known, Dirac's theorem (deg ≥ n/2), Ore's theorem, why bipartite graphs with unequal sides have no Hamilton cycle, travelling salesman link, Gray codes on Qn
12 Trees and their properties: tree definitions that are all equivalent (connected acyclic, n - 1 edges, unique path), leaves (every tree with n ≥ 2 has at least 2 leaves), forests (n - k edges), centre of a tree, rooted trees recap
13 Spanning trees and counting them: spanning tree exists iff connected, fundamental cycles, Cayley's formula n^(n-2) labelled trees, Prufer code (encode and decode), Kirchhoff matrix-tree theorem (Laplacian D - A, any cofactor), worked K4 = 16
14 Minimum spanning trees, the theory: cut property and cycle property, why greedy works, uniqueness when weights are distinct, Kruskal and Prim recapped as applications, Boruvka idea, worked example, real uses (laying cables, roads)
15 Shortest paths, the theory: weighted distance, optimal substructure (sub-paths of shortest paths are shortest), BFS for unweighted, Dijkstra recap with a worked example, negative edges and cycles, all-pairs Floyd-Warshall idea, shortest path trees
16 Planar graphs: plane drawing, faces, Euler's formula v - e + f = 2, corollaries e ≤ 3v - 6 and e ≤ 2v - 4 (no triangles), K5 and K3,3 are non-planar, every planar graph has a vertex of degree ≤ 5, Platonic solids
17 Kuratowski and planarity testing: subdivisions, Kuratowski's theorem, Wagner's minor version, finding a K3,3 subdivision in the Petersen graph, dual graphs, linear-time planarity tests exist (named), crossing number idea
18 Vertex colouring: proper colouring, chromatic number χ, χ of Kn, Cn (2 or 3), bipartite, trees, clique number ω ≤ χ, greedy colouring and χ ≤ Δ + 1, Brooks' theorem (statement), welsh-powell order, exam-timetable example
19 Chromatic polynomial and the four colour theorem: P(G, k) for trees k(k-1)^(n-1), Kn, deletion-contraction, map colouring and duals, five colour theorem proof idea, Four Colour Theorem history (Appel and Haken 1976, computer proof)
20 Edge colouring: proper edge colouring, chromatic index χ', Vizing's theorem (Δ or Δ + 1), König's line colouring theorem for bipartite graphs, χ'(Kn) depends on n odd/even, round-robin tournament scheduling
21 Bipartite graphs and matchings: bipartite iff no odd cycle (2-colouring by BFS), matching, maximal vs maximum vs perfect, augmenting paths (Berge's theorem), vertex cover, König's theorem (max matching = min vertex cover in bipartite)
22 Hall's marriage theorem and assignment: Hall's condition |N(S)| ≥ |S|, worked failing example, regular bipartite graphs have perfect matchings, independent sets, stable marriage (Gale-Shapley) idea, Hungarian method named
23 Network flows: flow network, capacity and conservation, cuts and cut capacity, max-flow min-cut theorem, augmenting paths in the residual graph (Ford-Fulkerson recap), integrality, matching via flow, Menger via flow
24 Directed graphs, DAGs, tournaments and Ramsey: DAG iff topological order, longest path in DAG (project scheduling), tournaments always have a Hamilton path, king vertex, Ramsey R(3,3) = 6 party problem, R(4,4) = 18, why big Ramsey numbers are unknown
25 Applications and wrap-up: social networks, maps and GPS, web PageRank idea, scheduling, chemistry (isomers), circuits, compilers (register allocation by colouring), recap of the course, where to go next (algorithms, networks, AI, research)
