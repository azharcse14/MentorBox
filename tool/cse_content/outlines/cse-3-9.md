# Course: Graph Theory  (level id "deep-cse-3-9", lesson ids "deep-cse-3-9-01".."-25")
Builds on Discrete Mathematics (deep-cse-1-3, lessons 23-25: graph basics, Euler/Hamilton intro, trees) and Algorithms
(deep-cse-3-1, lessons 18-22: BFS/DFS, MST, shortest paths, max flow). Those algorithms are only recapped; here the focus is
the mathematics of graphs: definitions, theorems, proofs (in simple words), counting, and worked examples on small graphs.
01 What graph theory is: Konigsberg bridges story, graph as dots and lines, G = (V, E), order and size, modelling real problems (roads, friends, molecules, timetables), the big questions of the course, course map
02 Basic terms and kinds of graphs: simple graph, multigraph, loops, pseudograph, directed graph, weighted graph, adjacent, incident, neighbourhood N(v), degree, isolated and pendant vertices, minimum and maximum degree, regular graphs, trivial and null graphs
03 Degrees and degree sequences: handshaking lemma proof, odd-degree vertices come in even number, in-degree/out-degree sum, degree sequence, graphic sequences, Havel-Hakimi algorithm worked, Erdos-Gallai condition stated, party puzzles
04 Special graph families: path Pn, cycle Cn, complete Kn with n(n-1)/2 edges, wheel Wn, complete bipartite Km,n, star, hypercube Qn (2^n vertices, n*2^(n-1) edges), Petersen graph, counting their edges and degrees
05 Subgraphs and graph operations: subgraph, spanning subgraph, induced subgraph, vertex and edge deletion, complement and self-complementary graphs, union, join, Cartesian product, line graph, edge contraction
06 Representing graphs with matrices and lists: adjacency matrix, incidence matrix, adjacency list, edge list, space and speed trade-offs, degree from rows, A squared and counting walks, degree matrix and Laplacian L = D - A
07 Graph isomorphism: same shape different labels, bijection that keeps edges, invariants (order, size, degree sequence, cycles, bipartite), proving isomorphic with a mapping, proving non-isomorphic with an invariant, automorphisms, 11 graphs on 4 vertices, why it is hard in general
08 Walks, trails, paths, cycles and distance: definitions and differences, open/closed, every u-v walk contains a u-v path, distance, eccentricity, radius, diameter, centre, girth, circumference, worked examples
09 Connectivity basics: connected graphs and components, at least n-k edges, cut vertices, bridges (an edge is a bridge iff it is on no cycle), blocks, connectivity in digraphs (strong, weak), condensation
10 Vertex and edge connectivity: kappa and lambda, Whitney's inequality kappa <= lambda <= delta, k-connected graphs, Whitney's 2-connected theorem (two disjoint paths, common cycle), Menger's theorem, network reliability
11 Bipartite graphs: definition and 2-colouring, Konig's theorem (bipartite iff no odd cycle) with proof idea, BFS test, edges at most n^2/4, real uses (jobs and workers, students and courses)
12 Eulerian graphs: Konigsberg solved, Euler circuit and trail, Euler's theorem (connected, all degrees even) with proof idea, exactly two odd vertices for a trail, Fleury's and Hierholzer's algorithms worked, directed version, Chinese postman idea
13 Hamiltonian graphs: Hamilton path and cycle, Dirac's and Ore's theorems, a necessary condition (remove k vertices, at most k components), Petersen graph and Km,n cases, Gray code on Qn, knight's tour, TSP and NP-completeness
14 Trees I, what makes a tree: equivalent definitions (connected acyclic, n-1 edges, unique paths, minimally connected, maximally acyclic), at least two leaves, forests with n-k edges, centre of a tree (one or two vertices), rooted trees vocabulary
15 Trees II, counting trees: labelled vs unlabelled trees, Cayley's formula n^(n-2), Prufer code encoding and decoding worked, matrix-tree theorem with a worked example, spanning trees of K4 and cycles
16 Spanning trees, cycles and cuts: spanning trees and cotrees, fundamental cycles, cyclomatic number m-n+1, edge cuts and bonds, cut property and cycle property of MSTs, unique MST with distinct weights, Kruskal/Prim/Boruvka recap
17 Directed graphs and tournaments: digraph terms, DAGs and topological order (a DAG has a source and a sink), strongly connected components, tournaments, every tournament has a Hamilton path (Redei), kings, score sequences, round-robin cricket example
18 Matchings and Hall's theorem: matching, maximal vs maximum, perfect matching, alternating and augmenting paths, Berge's theorem, Hall's marriage theorem with examples, k-regular bipartite graphs have perfect matchings
19 Covers, independent sets and stable matchings: vertex cover, edge cover, independent set, Konig-Egervary theorem, Gallai identities, Tutte's theorem stated, stable marriage problem and Gale-Shapley algorithm worked
20 Planar graphs and Euler's formula: drawing without crossings, plane graph, faces, Euler's formula V - E + F = 2 with induction idea, E <= 3V - 6 and E <= 2V - 4, K5 and K3,3 are not planar, every planar graph has a vertex of degree at most 5
21 Kuratowski, duals and crossings: subdivisions and Kuratowski's theorem, Wagner's minors version, testing a graph, dual graphs, platonic solids from Euler's formula, crossing number, real uses (circuit boards, metro maps)
22 Vertex colouring: proper colouring, chromatic number, chi of Kn, Cn, trees, bipartite graphs, bounds clique <= chi <= Delta + 1, greedy and Welsh-Powell, Brooks' theorem, exam timetabling and register allocation
23 Chromatic polynomials and the four colour theorem: counting colourings, P(Kn), trees, cycles, deletion-contraction worked, map colouring via duals, five colour theorem idea, four colour theorem history (1852 to 1976, computer proof)
24 Edge colouring: proper edge colouring, chromatic index, Vizing's theorem (Delta or Delta + 1), Konig's edge colouring theorem for bipartite graphs, K_n class 1 or 2, round-robin scheduling, Petersen graph
25 Cliques, independence, Ramsey and Turan + course wrap-up: clique number, independence number, complement link, R(3,3) = 6 party proof, known Ramsey numbers, Mantel and Turan theorems, course recap and what to learn next
