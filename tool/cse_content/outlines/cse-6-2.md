# Course: Computer Security & Cryptography  (level id "deep-cse-6-2", lesson ids "deep-cse-6-2-01".."-26")
Builds on Discrete Mathematics (deep-cse-1-3: lessons 13-15 modular arithmetic, Euclid, modular inverse, RSA idea), Operating Systems
(deep-cse-4-1: lesson 25 protection, access matrix, rwx), Computer Networks (deep-cse-4-3: lesson 26 security overview and TLS idea),
Web Engineering (deep-cse-5-3: lessons 20-22 sessions/JWT, password hashing, XSS/SQLi/CSRF/HTTPS) and Software Testing (deep-cse-5-8:
lesson 23 security testing). Those are only recapped; here we go deeper into how and why the mechanisms work.
Later courses: Professional Ethics & Cyber Law (deep-cse-6-6) owns law and ethics, Advanced Cyber Security (deep-cse-9-5) owns
offensive techniques, forensics and advanced attacks, Blockchain (deep-cse-9-2) owns hash chains and consensus: mention and point there.
Defensive and educational only: attacks are explained as ideas, never as step-by-step exploits. Every worked number is checked in Python.
01 What computer security is: why it matters, assets, CIA triad (confidentiality, integrity, availability) with examples, authenticity, non-repudiation, accountability, security as people + process + technology, course map
02 Threats, vulnerabilities, attacks and risk: asset, threat, vulnerability, exploit, attack surface, passive vs active attacks (interception, interruption, modification, fabrication), attacker models (outsider, insider, skill and resources), risk = likelihood x impact worked, risk treatment (reduce, transfer, avoid, accept), STRIDE threat modelling
03 Security principles: Saltzer and Schroeder (least privilege, fail-safe defaults, economy of mechanism, complete mediation, open design, separation of privilege, least common mechanism, psychological acceptability), Kerckhoffs's principle, security through obscurity, defence in depth, security vs usability trade-off
04 Cryptography basics and substitution ciphers: plaintext, ciphertext, key, encryption, decryption, cryptology vs cryptanalysis, attack models (ciphertext-only, known-plaintext, chosen-plaintext, chosen-ciphertext), Caesar worked, brute force over 25 keys, monoalphabetic substitution and 26! keys, frequency analysis worked
05 Polyalphabetic and transposition ciphers: Vigenere worked (ATTACKATDAWN / LEMON), why it hides frequencies, Kasiski and key-length idea, Playfair brief, rail fence and columnar transposition worked, Enigma history brief, lessons from classical crypto
06 Perfect secrecy and stream ciphers: XOR worked, one-time pad, Shannon's perfect secrecy, why OTP is impractical, two-time pad danger, stream cipher idea (keystream from a key), PRNG vs CSPRNG, RC4 broken and ChaCha20 today, confusion and diffusion
07 Block ciphers, Feistel networks and DES: block vs stream, ideal block cipher and why it is impossible, Feistel round worked with a toy function, decryption reuses the structure, DES (64-bit block, 56-bit key, 16 rounds, 1977), brute force and EFF 1998, double DES and meet-in-the-middle, 3DES
08 AES: NIST competition and Rijndael (2001), 128-bit block, 128/192/256-bit keys with 10/12/14 rounds, state as 4x4 bytes, SubBytes, ShiftRows, MixColumns, AddRoundKey, key expansion idea, last round, why AES is fast (tables, AES-NI), brute force arithmetic for 2^128
09 Modes of operation: why modes, ECB and the penguin problem, CBC with IV and chaining, PKCS#7 padding worked, padding oracle idea (concept only), CTR mode, IV/nonce rules, authenticated encryption, GCM concept, ChaCha20-Poly1305, encrypt-then-MAC
10 Number theory for crypto I: modular arithmetic recap, congruence, Z_n, gcd and Euclid worked, extended Euclid worked (17 and 3120), modular inverse, when an inverse exists, multiplicative group Z_n* (recap of deep-cse-1-3)
11 Number theory for crypto II: primes and why big primes are easy to find, Fermat's little theorem with worked check, Euler's phi worked, Euler's theorem, fast modular exponentiation (square-and-multiply) worked, primality testing idea (Fermat test, Miller-Rabin), CRT brief
12 Public-key cryptography and RSA: key distribution problem, one-way and trapdoor functions, RSA key generation worked (p=61, q=53), encrypt and decrypt worked (m=65), why it works (Euler), security from factoring, key sizes, textbook RSA problems and OAEP, hybrid encryption
13 Diffie-Hellman and the discrete logarithm: shared secret over a public channel, paint-mixing analogy, DH worked (p=23, g=5), discrete log problem, man-in-the-middle on unauthenticated DH, ephemeral DH and forward secrecy, ElGamal brief
14 Elliptic curve cryptography (concept level): curve equation, point addition idea, scalar multiplication, a tiny curve over a small prime field, ECDLP, ECDH and ECDSA, key size comparison (256-bit ECC vs 3072-bit RSA), Curve25519 and P-256 names
15 Hash functions: what a hash is, fixed output, avalanche, preimage, second-preimage and collision resistance, birthday paradox worked (23 people, 2^(n/2)), MD5 and SHA-1 broken, SHA-2 and SHA-3 families, Merkle-Damgard idea and length extension, uses (integrity, passwords recap, blockchain pointer)
16 MACs and HMAC: integrity plus authenticity, why a plain hash is not enough, MAC definition, HMAC construction, CBC-MAC brief, verification and constant-time compare, replay attacks with nonces, counters and timestamps, MAC vs signature preview
17 Digital signatures: handwritten vs digital, sign with private key and verify with public key, hash-then-sign, RSA signature worked with small numbers, DSA, ECDSA and Ed25519 names, nonce reuse danger (concept), non-repudiation, MAC vs signature table
18 PKI and certificates: the "whose public key is this" problem, certificate authorities, X.509 fields, chain of trust and root stores, validation steps, revocation (CRL, OCSP), Let's Encrypt and ACME, certificate transparency, web of trust (PGP)
19 TLS from the crypto view: goals, TLS 1.3 handshake step by step (key share, certificate, CertificateVerify, Finished), key derivation idea (HKDF), record protection with AEAD, forward secrecy, what TLS 1.3 removed vs 1.2, downgrade protection, HSTS recap, what TLS does not protect
20 Authentication: identification vs authentication, factors (know, have, are), passwords and entropy worked, storage recap (salt, slow hash), online vs offline attacks, credential stuffing, MFA, HOTP and TOTP idea, SMS OTP weaknesses, biometrics (FAR, FRR, EER), passkeys and FIDO2 concept
21 Access control: authorization, subjects/objects/rights, access matrix, ACLs vs capabilities, DAC, MAC with Bell-LaPadula (no read up, no write down) and Biba, RBAC with roles worked, ABAC brief, Unix permissions recap, reference monitor
22 Software security: why bugs become vulnerabilities, memory layout recap, buffer overflow concept (no exploit steps), memory safety and memory-safe languages, defences (bounds checks, stack canaries, ASLR, NX/DEP), integer overflow, input validation, injection recap, TOCTOU race, secure coding habits, SAST and fuzzing pointer
23 Malware: virus, worm, trojan, ransomware, spyware and stalkerware, adware, rootkit, keylogger, botnet, infection vectors, famous cases (Morris worm 1988, WannaCry 2017), detection (signatures, heuristics, behaviour, sandboxing), defences
24 Social engineering and phishing: why humans are targeted, phishing, spear phishing, smishing, vishing, pretexting, baiting, mobile-money and OTP scam patterns (generic), fake apps, red flags, technical defences (SPF/DKIM/DMARC recap, MFA), awareness, what to do if scammed
25 Network security: firewalls (packet filter, stateful, application proxy, next-gen), rules worked, DMZ, IDS vs IPS, signature vs anomaly detection, false positives and negatives, VPNs (IPsec, TLS VPN, WireGuard), DoS and DDoS, Wi-Fi security (WEP broken, WPA2, WPA3)
26 Security in practice and capstone: patching, backups (3-2-1), logging and monitoring, incident response phases, post-quantum note (Shor, NIST 2024 standards, hedged), capstone securing a Bangladesh mobile-wallet style app end to end, course recap and what next (deep-cse-6-6, deep-cse-9-5, deep-cse-9-2)
