# Course: Digital Signal Processing  (level id "deep-cse-5-7", lesson ids "deep-cse-5-7-01".."-26")
Builds on Signals & Systems (deep-cse-3-7: CT signals, LTI basics, convolution, Fourier series/transform, Laplace, first look at
sampling, DTFT and z), Math III (deep-cse-2-7: complex numbers, Euler, partial fractions) and Data Communication (deep-cse-3-6:
dB, Nyquist/Shannon, modulation). Those are only recapped. Here the focus is the DISCRETE side done in depth and by hand: sampling
and quantization numbers, discrete convolution, difference equations, z-transform, DTFT/DFT/FFT, digital filter design (FIR window,
IIR bilinear), structures, multirate, fixed-point hardware and real applications. Every worked number is checked with a script.
01 What DSP is: signals as numbers, the DSP chain (sensor, anti-alias filter, ADC, processor, DAC, reconstruction filter), where DSP lives (phones, music, voice calls, medical, radar, images), why digital wins, recap of what Signals & Systems already gave us, course map
02 Analog vs digital and the ADC/DAC chain: continuous vs discrete in time and amplitude, sample-and-hold, ADC types in one line each (flash, SAR, sigma-delta), DAC and zero-order hold, reconstruction filter, advantages and costs of digital
03 Sampling theorem and aliasing worked: fs > 2 fmax, Nyquist rate vs Nyquist frequency, folding, alias = |f - k fs| (7 kHz at 10 kHz looks like 3 kHz, identical samples), anti-aliasing filter, 8 kHz telephone, 44.1/48 kHz audio, wagon wheel, oversampling
04 Quantization and SNR: levels 2^N, step size, rounding error up to step/2, uniform vs non-uniform (mu-law/A-law brief), SQNR = 6.02N + 1.76 dB worked (8, 12, 16 bit), bit rate = fs x N x channels (CD 1,411,200 bps, phone 64 kbps), dither idea
05 Discrete-time signals and operations: x[n], impulse, step, ramp, exponential, sinusoid, digital frequency w = 2 pi f / fs, DT periodicity rule, shift/fold/downsample, energy and power (0.5^n u[n] energy 4/3), even/odd parts
06 Discrete-time systems and properties: memory, causality, linearity, time invariance, BIBO stability, invertibility, testing y = 2x+3, y = n x[n], y = x[n+1], y = x[2n], accumulator, worked tests
07 LTI systems and discrete convolution by hand: signal as sum of shifted impulses, y = sum x[k] h[n-k], flip-and-slide and table methods, [1,2,3]*[1,1,1] = [1,3,6,5,3], output length, properties, moving average
08 Difference equations and impulse response: linear constant-coefficient difference equations, recursive vs non-recursive, initial rest, iterating y[n] = 0.5 y[n-1] + x[n] (impulse 1, 0.5, 0.25...; step 1, 1.5, 1.75...), block diagrams with delay/adder/multiplier
09 FIR vs IIR: finite vs infinite impulse response, moving average and echo (FIR), reverb and first-order smoother (IIR), stability, phase, cost per sample (multiplies x fs), when to choose which
10 The z-transform and ROC: definition X(z) = sum x[n] z^-n, z = r e^jw, finite sequences, a^n u[n] and -a^n u[-n-1] (same formula, different ROC), ROC rules, properties (linearity, shift = z^-k, convolution = product), link to DTFT on the unit circle
11 Inverse z-transform: inspection, long division (power series), partial fractions worked: 1/((1-0.5z^-1)(1-0.25z^-1)) gives 2(0.5)^n - (0.25)^n, checked by recursion, choosing sequences by ROC
12 System function, poles, zeros and stability: H(z) = Y(z)/X(z) from a difference equation, pole-zero plot, causal + stable iff all poles inside unit circle, worked poles 0.5 and 0.4, unstable 1.2, solving a difference equation with z
13 Frequency response: H(e^jw) as H(z) on the unit circle, magnitude and phase, dB, geometric view (near a zero dips, near a pole peaks), 2-point average |cos(w/2)|, first-order smoother 0.1/(1-0.9e^-jw) worked, mapping w to Hz, group delay
14 The DTFT: definition and inverse, periodic with 2 pi, symmetry for real signals, DTFT of a^n u[n] and of a rectangular pulse, key properties, Parseval, why we cannot compute it directly (continuous w, infinite sum)
15 The DFT: sampling the DTFT at N points, X[k] = sum x[n] e^-j2pi kn/N, worked 4-point [1,2,3,4] -> [10, -2+2j, -2, -2-2j], inverse DFT check, bin frequency k fs/N, resolution fs/N, conjugate symmetry, matrix view
16 The FFT: why direct DFT costs N^2, radix-2 decimation in time, twiddle factors, butterfly, worked 4-point FFT, (N/2)log2 N multiplications (1024: 1,048,576 vs 5,120, about 205x), bit reversal, inverse FFT trick, where FFT runs
17 Circular vs linear convolution: product of DFTs = circular convolution, [1,2,3] and [1,1] circular N=3 gives [4,3,5] vs linear [1,3,5,3], zero padding to L+M-1, fast convolution via FFT, overlap-add and overlap-save idea
18 Spectral analysis with the DFT: frequency resolution, spectral leakage when a tone falls between bins, windows (rectangular, Hann, Hamming) and their sidelobes, zero padding (smoother picture, not more resolution), spectrogram/STFT, reading a phone audio spectrum
19 Digital filter basics and linear phase: low/high/band-pass/band-stop, specs (passband, stopband, transition band, ripple, attenuation in dB), ideal filters are non-causal, phase and group delay, linear phase from symmetric FIR (h = [1,2,1] delay 1), four FIR types brief
20 FIR design by windowing: ideal low-pass h_d[n] = sin(wc(n-a))/(pi(n-a)), truncation and Gibbs, rectangular/Hann/Hamming windows (sidelobe and stopband numbers), worked 7-tap Hamming low-pass at wc = pi/2, choosing length from transition width (N about 3.3/df), frequency sampling and Parks-McClellan named only
21 IIR design via the bilinear transform: analog prototypes (Butterworth idea), s = (2/T)(1-z^-1)/(1+z^-1), frequency warping Omega = (2/T)tan(w/2), worked pre-warp fs = 8 kHz, fc = 1 kHz -> first-order H(z) = 0.2929(1+z^-1)/(1-0.4142z^-1), impulse invariance named, FIR vs IIR comparison
22 Filter structures: direct form I and II, transposed form, cascade of second-order sections, parallel form, FIR transversal (tapped delay line), counting delays and multiplies, why cascade is safer with finite precision
23 Multirate DSP: decimation (filter then keep every Mth sample), interpolation (insert zeros then filter), rational change L/M (44.1 to 48 kHz = 160/147), polyphase idea, oversampling ADCs, uses in audio and phones
24 DSP hardware and fixed-point: DSP processors (MAC unit, Harvard architecture, circular buffers, zero-overhead loops), microcontrollers with DSP instructions, FPGA, GPU; Q15 format worked, overflow vs saturation, rounding vs truncation, coefficient quantization, limit cycles idea, scaling
25 Applications: audio (equalizer, noise cancellation, compression idea), speech (framing, LPC idea), images (2D convolution kernels, JPEG 8x8 DCT), communications (OFDM via FFT in Wi-Fi/4G), biomedical (ECG noise types, heart rate from R-R interval), radar/sonar brief
26 Capstone and wrap-up: removing 50 Hz mains hum from an ECG sampled at 500 Hz: notch filter zeros at e^(+-j0.2pi), poles at r = 0.95, coefficients, gain normalisation, checked response (0 at 50 Hz, about 8 Hz wide), harmonics at 100 Hz, Python/MATLAB pipeline, course recap and what next
