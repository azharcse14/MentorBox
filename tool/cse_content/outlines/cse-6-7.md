# Course: Final Year Project / Thesis  (level id "deep-cse-6-7", lesson ids "deep-cse-6-7-01".."-25")
A how-to course for running a final year project or thesis end to end: picking a topic and supervisor, reading and reviewing the
literature, stating the problem, writing the proposal, choosing a method, planning, building or experimenting, evaluating honestly,
writing the thesis book and surviving the defence. Every lesson follows one invented running example where useful (a rotten-mango
detector and a campus bus tracker) plus small worked numbers (topic scoring, hour budgets, PERT estimates, risk scores, Cohen's kappa,
precision/recall/F1, p95 latency, SUS score, mean and standard deviation, slide timing) checked with Python. Builds on Technical Writing
(deep-cse-6-8: 03 writing process, 10 figures and tables, 18-19 reports, 20 proposals, 21 papers and thesis, 22 citing, 23 plagiarism and AI,
24 presentations and defence), Software Engineering (deep-cse-4-4), Software Testing (deep-cse-5-8), Probability & Statistics (deep-cse-2-6),
Machine Learning (deep-cse-6-1), HCI (deep-cse-5-5). Those writing skills are only recapped; here the focus is running the work itself.
Point forward instead of re-teaching: Professional Ethics & Cyber Law (deep-cse-6-6), Software Project Management (deep-cse-6-11),
Research Methodology (deep-cse-7-7). University rules (credits, page limits, marking split, board make-up) differ, so they stay generic:
"check your department's handbook". Tools (Git, Overleaf, Zotero, Colab, Google Scholar) appear only as version-neutral examples.
01 What a final year project or thesis is: definition, project vs thesis vs industry-attachment style, what it proves, typical shape in Bangladeshi universities (spread over two or three terms, solo or small team, supervisor, board), deliverables (proposal, progress reports, code/data, thesis book, defence), how it is judged, the journey map, course map
02 Mindset, skills and time: from courses to self-driven work, owner mindset, what you already know (which earlier courses help), hours budget worked (weeks x hours), weekly rhythm, balancing with other courses and job hunting, motivation dips and the research "valley", keeping a project diary
03 Finding and choosing a topic: idea sources (problems around you, supervisor lists, papers' future work, industry, datasets, competitions), area -> topic -> question funnel, criteria (interest, size, data, skills, supervisor fit, novelty, usefulness), weighted scoring matrix worked, feasibility check, red flags (too big, no data, hardware you cannot get, hype words)
04 Choosing and working with a supervisor: how allocation usually works (generic), finding a fit (their papers and past students), first contact email worked, preparing for meetings, agenda and minutes, meeting cadence, receiving feedback, co-supervisors, what supervisors can and cannot do, when things go wrong
05 Working as a team: team size and roles, choosing teammates, team contract, splitting work by component not by chapter, Git collaboration and code review, contribution log, handling free-riders and conflict, how individual marks are usually judged, solo vs team trade-offs
06 Finding the literature: what counts (journals, conferences, preprints, theses, books, grey literature), where to search (Google Scholar, IEEE Xplore, ACM DL, arXiv, Semantic Scholar, university library), keywords and Boolean search strings worked, backward and forward snowballing, judging quality (peer review, venue, recency, citations with care, predatory journals), getting access legally
07 Reading papers efficiently: anatomy recap (point to deep-cse-6-8-21), three-pass reading, what to note, extraction matrix (paper, problem, method, data, metric, result, limitation), reading result tables critically, critical questions, reading speed and quantity worked, reference manager habits
08 Writing the literature review: summary vs synthesis, organising by theme/approach/time, comparison table, finding the gap (types: knowledge, method, data/context, performance, evaluation), narrative vs systematic review, PRISMA-style counts worked, linking review to your problem, common mistakes
09 Problem statement, research questions and objectives: problem statement formula (context, problem, evidence, consequence, gap), research questions (descriptive, comparative, causal, design), hypotheses (null and alternative), aim vs objectives, SMART recap, scope and delimitations, assumptions, contributions list, alignment check
10 The thesis proposal: recap proposal writing (deep-cse-6-8-20), thesis proposal sections, preliminary results, expected outcomes, timeline and resources, worked mini proposal for the mango detector, proposal presentation or pre-defence, reasons proposals get sent back, changing the plan later
11 Research design and methodology: what methodology means, quantitative, qualitative, mixed, experimental and quasi-experimental, design science (build an artifact, evaluate it), survey, case study, simulation, benchmarking, variables (independent, dependent, controlled), choosing a design from the research question, methodology chapter preview
12 Planning the work: work breakdown structure, milestones and deliverables, Gantt chart, three-point (PERT) estimates worked, dependencies and critical path idea, buffers, weekly plans and Kanban board, progress reports, re-planning, point to deep-cse-6-11
13 Risks and research ethics: common project risks, risk register with likelihood x impact worked, mitigation and contingency, scope control (MoSCoW), ethics with human participants (informed consent, privacy, anonymisation, minors, harm), ethics review boards, data protection, using other people's code and data (licences), point to deep-cse-6-6
14 Data collection and preparation: public datasets vs own data, collection plans (photos, surveys, logs, sensors), how much data (per class counts worked), labelling guidelines, inter-annotator agreement (Cohen's kappa worked), cleaning, splitting (train/validation/test, stratified), data leakage traps, dataset documentation, storage and backup, local Bangla/Bangladesh data
15 Building the system (project track): requirements recap (deep-cse-4-4), architecture and tech choices with reasons, minimum viable product first, iterations, version control workflow, code quality habits, testing strategy (point to deep-cse-5-8), deployment and demo environment, documentation, campus bus tracker worked
16 Running experiments (thesis track): baseline first, controlled comparison (change one thing), ablation studies, hyperparameter tuning on validation only, random seeds and repeated runs (mean and standard deviation worked), experiment log, compute budget worked (GPU hours, free cloud notebooks), sanity checks, when to stop
17 Tools and reproducibility: Git and remote backups, folder structure, environment files and pinned versions, notebooks vs scripts, experiment tracking, configuration files, seeds, the 3-2-1 backup rule, Overleaf/LaTeX and Word, reference managers (Zotero) and BibTeX, a reproducibility checklist
18 Evaluation I, choosing and computing metrics: metric follows the question, confusion matrix, accuracy, precision, recall, F1 worked, class imbalance trap, regression MAE and RMSE worked, system metrics (latency mean vs p95 worked, throughput), usability with SUS worked, user studies basics
19 Evaluation II, fair comparison and statistics: same data and splits for all, k-fold cross-validation, why one number is not enough, standard deviation and confidence interval worked, significance testing idea (p-value meaning, paired test), effect size, multiple comparisons, threats to validity (internal, external, construct, conclusion), point to deep-cse-2-6 and deep-cse-7-7
20 Analysing and presenting results: results vs discussion, tables and figures recap (deep-cse-6-8-10), error analysis (sample failures, per-class results), explaining why, negative and unexpected results, honest limitations, avoiding overclaiming, answering the research questions explicitly
21 The thesis book I, structure: front matter (title page, declaration, certificate/approval, acknowledgements, abstract, contents, lists of figures/tables/abbreviations), chapter plan (introduction, background and literature review, methodology/system design, implementation, results and discussion, conclusion and future work), references, appendices, project-style vs research-style books, formatting and templates (generic), page budget worked
22 The thesis book II, writing it: write as you go, chapter order (methods first, introduction and abstract last), daily word targets worked, drafting with LaTeX/Overleaf or Word, figures and citations recap (deep-cse-6-8-22), supervisor feedback rounds, the abstract, similarity check and honest AI-tool use recap (deep-cse-6-8-23), final checks, printing, binding and submission
23 Presentation, demo and defence (viva): what the board looks for, typical board make-up (generic), slide plan and timing worked, demo preparation (backup video, offline mode), rehearsal, typical questions, answering technique (pause, repeat, answer, evidence), "I don't know" honestly, team answers, after the defence (corrections, final submission)
24 Common pitfalls and how to recover: late start, scope creep, coding before reading, no baseline, data that never arrives, lost work, results that do not improve, p-hacking and cherry-picking, teammate disappears, supervisor unreachable, last-week writing, plagiarism; warning signs and recovery plans; two rescue stories
25 After the thesis and capstone: publishing (choosing a venue, turning a thesis into a paper, conferences in Bangladesh and abroad, predatory venues recap), portfolio, GitHub and CV, using it for jobs and interviews, higher studies (SOP, recommendation letters), open-sourcing code and data, turning it into a product, capstone timeline for the mango detector end to end, course recap and what next (deep-cse-7-7)
