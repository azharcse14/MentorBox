# Course: Human Computer Interaction  (level id "deep-cse-5-5", lesson ids "deep-cse-5-5-01".."-27")
Builds on System Analysis & Design (deep-cse-4-6: its UI design lesson covered Shneiderman's eight golden rules, forms, messages and
fact-finding interviews/questionnaires from the analyst's side; here we go deeper from the user's side and only recap those) and
Software Engineering (deep-cse-4-4: process models and agile). Mobile App Development (deep-cse-5-4) runs in parallel: it builds the
app, this course decides what the app should be like for people. Focus: the human, the computer and the interaction between them;
Norman's principles; usability and UX; the user-centred design cycle (research, task analysis, ideation, prototyping, visual design);
evaluation (heuristics, walkthroughs, usability testing, SUS); predictive models (Fitts, Hick, KLM) with worked numbers;
accessibility; designing for Bangla, low-literacy and low-bandwidth users; ethics and emerging interfaces.
A running example appears now and then: "DaktarDekhao", a fictional Bangla-first app for booking a doctor's serial at a district hospital.
01 What HCI is and why it matters: definition, human + computer + interaction, a multidisciplinary field (CS, psychology, design, sociology), short history (Sketchpad 1963, Engelbart's mouse demo 1968, Xerox Alto, Macintosh 1984, the web, iPhone multi-touch 2007), cost of bad design, HCI vs UI vs UX, jobs, course map
02 The human I, perception: senses as input channels, how vision works in brief, visual acuity and reading, colour perception and colour vision deficiency, preattentive features (colour, size, orientation), Gestalt principles (proximity, similarity, closure, continuity, figure-ground, common region, common fate, symmetry) applied to screens, hearing and touch as channels
03 The human II, memory, attention and thinking: sensory, short-term/working and long-term memory, Miller's 7 plus or minus 2 and chunking (and why ~4 chunks is the modern view), recognition vs recall, attention (selective, divided), change and inattentional blindness, cognitive load, mental models and how users build them
04 The computer side, input and output: keyboards (QWERTY, Bangla layouts Bijoy and Avro phonetic), mouse, touchpad, touchscreen (fat finger, target sizes), stylus, voice, camera and sensors (accelerometer, GPS); displays (resolution, pixel density, dp/pt), sound, haptics, printers; device context (phone outdoors, ATM, kiosk)
05 Interaction styles and paradigms: command line, menus, form fill-in, direct manipulation, WIMP, touch and gestures, natural language and conversational UI, question-answer; paradigms through history (batch, time-sharing, personal computing, WIMP, web, mobile, ubiquitous computing); choosing a style for a user
06 Norman's design principles I: The Design of Everyday Things, affordances (Gibson, real vs perceived), signifiers, mapping (natural mapping, stove-top example), constraints (physical, cultural, semantic, logical), discoverability, "Norman doors", applying them to app screens
07 Norman's design principles II: feedback (immediate, informative, not too much), response time limits (0.1 s, 1 s, 10 s), conceptual models (designer's model, user's model, system image), the seven stages of action, gulf of execution and gulf of evaluation worked on an ATM and a mobile recharge
08 Human error and designing for error: slips vs mistakes (Norman), capture, description and mode errors, rule-based vs knowledge-based mistakes, forcing functions (interlock, lockin, lockout), undo, confirmations and their overuse, good error messages, Swiss cheese model in brief
09 Usability and user experience: ISO 9241-11 definition (effectiveness, efficiency, satisfaction, context of use), Nielsen's five quality components, usability goals vs user experience goals, utility vs usability, the UX honeycomb, Shneiderman's golden rules as a recap only, measurable usability requirements
10 The user-centred design process: why design is iterative, Gould and Lewis principles, ISO 9241-210 activities (understand context, specify requirements, produce solutions, evaluate), design thinking (empathize, define, ideate, prototype, test), double diamond, Agile UX, where UCD fits in the SDLC
11 User research I, asking: research goals and questions, qualitative vs quantitative, interviews (structured, semi-structured, unstructured; good and leading questions), surveys (question types, Likert scales, sampling, pilot), focus groups, consent and research ethics, biases (social desirability, confirmation)
12 User research II, watching: observation (direct, indirect), contextual inquiry (master-apprentice, context, partnership, interpretation, focus), ethnography in brief, diary studies, analytics and logs, affinity diagramming to synthesise findings
13 Personas, scenarios and journeys: persona (goals, behaviours, frustrations; primary vs secondary; data-based not imagined), scenarios (problem vs activity scenarios), user stories and acceptance criteria, empathy maps, customer journey maps with touchpoints and pain points
14 Task analysis: why analyse tasks, goals vs tasks vs actions, hierarchical task analysis (HTA) worked for sending money from a mobile wallet, plans (fixed sequence, selective, optional), the stopping rule, tabular vs diagram HTA, using HTA to find problems, cognitive task analysis in brief
15 Ideation and sketching: problem statements and "How might we" questions, brainstorming rules, Crazy 8s, SCAMPER in brief, sketching vs drawing, sketching vocabulary, storyboards, choosing ideas (dot voting, impact/effort)
16 Prototyping and wireframes: what a prototype is for, low-fidelity (paper, card-based, Wizard of Oz), wireframes, medium and high fidelity, horizontal vs vertical prototypes, throw-away vs evolutionary, tools (Figma and similar), fidelity trade-offs, testing a paper prototype
17 Information architecture and navigation: IA components (organisation, labelling, navigation, search), organisation schemes (alphabetical, chronological, topic, task, audience), sitemaps, card sorting (open, closed) and tree testing, navigation patterns (tabs, bottom bar, drawer, breadcrumbs), depth vs breadth
18 Visual design I, layout and hierarchy: why visual design is functional, grids and alignment, white space, visual hierarchy (size, weight, colour, position), Gestalt in layout, F and Z reading patterns, consistency, mobile layout, worked redesign of a cluttered screen
19 Visual design II, typography, colour and icons: typefaces (serif, sans serif), size, line length and line height, Bangla fonts and conjunct rendering, colour meaning and culture, colour harmony and the 60-30-10 idea, icons with labels, design systems and guidelines (Material Design, Apple HIG)
20 Heuristic evaluation: what inspection methods are, Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics each with an example, running an evaluation (3-5 evaluators, independent then merge), severity ratings 0-4, writing findings, strengths and limits
21 Cognitive walkthrough and other inspections: the cognitive walkthrough (task, action sequence, user profile, the four questions) worked on a bus-ticket app, pluralistic walkthrough, guideline and standards review, consistency inspection, comparing inspection with user testing
22 Usability testing: goals, participants and recruiting, writing tasks, think-aloud (concurrent, retrospective), moderated vs unmoderated, lab vs remote, metrics (task success, time on task, errors), the five-user rule (1-(1-0.31)^n), System Usability Scale scoring worked (85), A/B testing in brief
23 Predictive models: Fitts' law (MT = a + b log2(D/W + 1)) worked, Hick-Hyman law (RT = a + b log2(n + 1)) worked, GOMS and the keystroke-level model (K, P, B, H, M) worked on a phone-number form, what models can and cannot predict
24 Accessibility: disability as mismatch, types (visual, hearing, motor, cognitive; permanent, temporary, situational), WCAG 2.2 and POUR, levels A/AA/AAA, contrast ratio worked (4.5:1), alt text, screen readers (TalkBack, VoiceOver, NVDA), keyboard access, touch target sizes, captions, inclusive design
25 Designing for Bangladesh: Bangla script and Unicode, fonts and conjuncts, input methods, Bangla vs English vs Banglish labels, digits and dates, low literacy (icons with labels, voice, video, simple flows), low bandwidth and older phones (lite apps, offline-first, small pages), USSD menus, shared phones, culture and trust
26 Dark patterns and ethics: persuasion vs manipulation, dark (deceptive) patterns (confirmshaming, roach motel, hidden costs, sneak into basket, forced continuity, trick questions, disguised ads, nagging, fake urgency), attention economy, privacy by design, consent, designer responsibility, ACM ethics in brief
27 Emerging interfaces and wrap-up: voice user interfaces, chatbots, AR, VR, wearables, gesture and tangible interfaces, ubiquitous computing, AI-powered interfaces and trust, what stays the same, course recap and what to learn next
