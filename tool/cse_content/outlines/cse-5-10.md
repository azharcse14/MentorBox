# Course: Game Development  (level id "deep-cse-5-10", lesson ids "deep-cse-5-10-01".."-26")
Builds on Computer Graphics (deep-cse-5-2: transformations, projection, z-buffer, lighting and shading, GPU pipeline, keyframes and lerp),
Artificial Intelligence (deep-cse-5-1: state-space search, A*, minimax), Human Computer Interaction (deep-cse-5-5: feedback, usability
testing, accessibility), Mobile App Development (deep-cse-5-4: Flutter/Dart, app lifecycle, stores), Computer Networks (deep-cse-4-3:
TCP/UDP, latency), Linear Algebra (deep-cse-2-5: vectors, dot/cross product) and Data Structures. Those are only recapped; here the focus is
the game-specific side: design, the game loop, engine architecture, 2D rendering, game math and physics, collision, game AI, procedural
content, performance, multiplayer, and the business and ethics of games. Multimedia Systems (deep-cse-5-9) covers audio/video formats and
compression in depth; lesson 20 only uses them. Engines (Unity, Unreal, Godot, Flame) are taught as concepts, no version numbers.
Pseudo-code is language-neutral; all worked numbers verified with a script. Original examples only; no existing game is recreated.
A running capstone appears in lesson 26: "Rickshaw Rush", a small original endless runner where a rickshaw dodges obstacles on a Dhaka road.
01 What a game is: rules, goals, feedback, play; games vs toys vs puzzles; voluntary challenge; why game dev is a CS course (graphics, AI, physics, networks, HCI together); team roles; course map
02 Game design I, the core: player verbs, mechanics, core loop and secondary loops, the MDA framework (mechanics, dynamics, aesthetics), rules as systems, paper prototyping, the game design document
03 Game design II, balance and motivation: difficulty curves, flow channel, risk vs reward, dominant strategies, balancing with numbers (DPS worked), intrinsic vs extrinsic motivation, Bartle's player types, rewards and schedules
04 Genres and platforms: action, platformer, puzzle, RPG, strategy (real-time and turn-based), simulation, sports and racing, casual, roguelike; PC, console, mobile, web, VR/AR; how platform shapes input, session length and design; mobile-first Bangladesh
05 The game loop: process input, update, render; frames, fps vs frame time; variable timestep with delta time worked; fixed timestep with an accumulator worked; spiral of death; interpolation; vsync; loop in pseudo-code
06 Game engines: what an engine gives (rendering, physics, audio, input, scenes, assets, tools), runtime vs editor, Unity, Unreal, Godot and Flutter's Flame as concepts, scripting languages, asset pipeline, build vs buy, choosing an engine
07 Game objects, scene graphs and ECS: deep inheritance problems, composition with components, scene graph with parent-child transforms worked, entity-component-system (entities as ids, components as data, systems as loops), cache-friendly data, events
08 Vectors and math for games: 2D vectors, screen y-down coordinates, length and normalize worked, diagonal speed bug, dot product for facing and field of view worked, 2D cross product sign for left/right, lerp worked, atan2, sin/cos for circles and bobbing
09 2D rendering: sprites, pivot, sprite sheets and texture atlases, frame animation timing worked, flipping, draw order and layers, tilemaps (grid, tile ids, memory worked, tile to pixel conversion), nine-slice; graphics pipeline recap
10 Cameras and screen space: world vs screen coordinates worked, camera follow with lerp and dead zone, bounds clamping, parallax layers worked, zoom, screen shake, resolutions and aspect ratios, letterboxing and safe areas
11 Input handling: polling vs events, keyboard, mouse, touch, gamepad, accelerometer; input mapping to actions; virtual joystick maths; dead zones; jump buffering and coyote time worked; input latency; accessibility and remapping
12 Physics I, motion: position, velocity, acceleration; explicit Euler worked; semi-implicit Euler; error vs step size worked; gravity and jump arcs (apex time and height worked, choosing gravity from jump height); variable jump height; friction and drag
13 Physics II, rigid bodies and engines: mass, force, F = ma, impulses, restitution and friction coefficients, static/kinematic/dynamic bodies, physics engines (Box2D, others) as concepts, tunnelling and continuous detection, fixed step and stability, when to fake physics
14 Collision detection I, shapes and tests: bounding volumes (AABB, circle, capsule, OBB), AABB overlap worked, circle overlap with squared distance worked, circle-vs-AABB by clamping, point tests, separating axis theorem idea, hitboxes vs hurtboxes
15 Collision detection II, broad and narrow phase: the n squared problem worked, broad vs narrow phase, uniform spatial grid worked, quadtrees, sweep and prune, collision layers and masks
16 Collision response: overlap vs response, minimum translation vector worked, resolving axis by axis for platformers, sliding along walls, bouncing with reflection v - 2(v.n)n and restitution worked, triggers, one-way platforms
17 Game AI I, decisions: what game AI wants (fun, not perfection), finite state machines (guard: patrol, chase, attack, return) with a transition table, hierarchical FSMs, behaviour trees (selector, sequence, conditions, actions, tick), utility AI briefly
18 Game AI II, movement: steering behaviours (seek, flee, arrive, wander) worked, combining forces, flocking idea, pathfinding on grids with A* recap and Manhattan heuristic worked, navigation meshes, path smoothing, cheating vs fair AI
19 Procedural generation: why, pseudo-random numbers and seeds, an LCG worked, reproducible levels, random walk and cellular automata caves, noise idea (value/Perlin), weighted loot tables worked, shuffle bags, designing with randomness
20 Audio, UI/HUD and game feel: sound effects vs music, channels and mixing, positional audio and panning, audio as feedback; HUD, diegetic UI, menus and screen flow, pause; game feel and juice (screen shake, particles, hit-stop); accessibility options
21 Saving, loading and game state: what state is, what to save, serialisation to JSON worked, save slots, autosave and checkpoints, versioning and migration, corruption and atomic writes, tamper checks, cloud saves, screen/state stacks
22 Performance: frame budget (16.7 ms at 60 fps, 33.3 ms at 30, 8.3 ms at 120) worked, CPU vs GPU bound, profiling, draw calls and batching, overdraw, object pooling with pseudo-code, garbage collection spikes, LOD and culling, mobile heat and battery
23 3D game basics: 3D coordinates and transforms recap, meshes, materials and textures, first- and third-person cameras, lighting and shadows recap, skeletal animation and blending, 3D physics and colliders, navmesh, level design in 3D
24 Multiplayer and networking: local vs online, peer-to-peer vs client-server, authoritative server, TCP vs UDP for games, tick rate, latency and jitter worked, client-side prediction, reconciliation, entity interpolation, lag compensation, cheating, matchmaking
25 Monetisation, ethics, playtesting and publishing: premium, free-to-play, ads, in-app purchases, subscriptions; loot boxes and gambling concerns; dark patterns; age ratings and children; playtesting methods and metrics; publishing to stores and the web; game dev in Bangladesh (skills, game jams, local themes)
26 Capstone, designing "Rickshaw Rush": concept and core loop, mechanics (lanes, dodge, collect), difficulty curve formula worked, game loop and state screens, ECS-style design, collision with AABBs, spawning with seeds and pooling, HUD and save of high score, playtest plan, course recap and what next
