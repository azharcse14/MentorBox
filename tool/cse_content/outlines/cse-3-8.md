# Course: Computer Peripherals & Interfacing  (level id "deep-cse-3-8", lesson ids "deep-cse-3-8-01".."-25")
Builds on Microprocessors & Assembly (deep-cse-3-3): 8085/8086 registers, IN/OUT, interrupts and the IVT, and a first look at 8255/8253/8259/8237/8251 are assumed, only recapped; here each chip is used in full interfacing designs.
Builds on Computer Architecture (deep-cse-3-2) for buses, memory hierarchy and I/O organisation, Digital Logic (deep-cse-2-3) for decoders, latches, buffers and timing, Electronic Devices (deep-cse-2-4) for diodes, LEDs, transistors as switches and op-amps.
Left for later courses: microcontroller firmware, RTOS and full embedded product design go to Embedded Systems (deep-cse-5-6); device drivers, interrupt handlers in the kernel and I/O scheduling in depth go to Operating Systems (deep-cse-4-1); line coding and modulation stay in Data Communication (deep-cse-3-6). Mention and move on.
Main chips: Intel 8086 with 8255 PPI, 8253/8254 PIT, 8259A PIC, 8237 DMAC, 8251 USART, ADC0804/0808, DAC0808.
01 What peripherals and interfacing are: CPU-memory-I/O picture, input/output/storage/communication devices, why devices cannot connect straight to the bus (speed, voltage, format), the interface circuit, course map
02 I/O basics and the I/O port: data, status and control registers, port addresses, buffers and latches (74LS244/74LS373), tri-state outputs, simple input switch and output LED port, handshaking idea
03 Port-mapped vs memory-mapped I/O: separate I/O space (IN/OUT, 64K ports on 8086, M/IO pin) vs memory addresses (any MOV works), pros and cons, address decoding for ports with 74LS138, worked port-address examples
04 Data transfer schemes: programmed I/O with polling (busy-wait loop, wasted CPU time worked example), interrupt-driven I/O, DMA, comparison table in words, when each fits
05 Interrupts for interfacing: recap of 8086 INTR/NMI, INTA cycle and vector number, ISR rules (save registers, short, EOI, IRET), latency, priorities, vectored vs polled interrupts, daisy chaining
06 The 8259A PIC in depth: pins and blocks (IRR, ISR, IMR, priority resolver), ICW1-ICW4 and OCW1-OCW3 worked for a PC (IRQ0 timer, IRQ1 keyboard, vector 08h), fully nested vs rotating priority, specific/non-specific EOI, master-slave cascade (15 IRQs in the PC/AT)
07 DMA and the 8237 controller: why DMA, HOLD/HLDA handshake, DREQ/DACK, address and count registers, single/block/demand modes, fly-by transfers, cycle stealing vs burst, PC uses (floppy, sound), bus mastering in modern PCs
08 The 8255 PPI in depth: ports A, B, C upper/lower, control word format, mode 0 simple I/O, mode 1 strobed I/O with STB/IBF/INTR and OBF/ACK, mode 2 bidirectional, BSR mode, worked control words, interfacing switches, LEDs and a relay
09 The 8253/8254 timer in depth: three 16-bit counters, CLK/GATE/OUT, control word, modes 0-5 with uses (interrupt on terminal count, one-shot, rate generator, square wave, software and hardware strobe), count calculation, PC timer at 1.193182 MHz (18.2 ticks/s, speaker tones), 8254 read-back
10 Serial communication basics: serial vs parallel, synchronous vs asynchronous, the UART frame (start, data, parity, stop), baud rate and frame time worked examples, error types (framing, parity, overrun), simplex/half/full duplex recap
11 RS-232 and the 8251 USART: RS-232 voltage levels (+3 to +15 V = 0, -3 to -15 V = 1), MAX232 level shifter, DTE/DCE, TxD RxD RTS CTS DTR DSR GND on DB-9, null modem, 8251 mode and command words, status register, the 16550 UART with FIFO, RS-485 note, USB-to-serial adapters
12 Parallel interfacing and the printer port: Centronics/IEEE 1284 port, data/status/control registers at 378h-37Ah, STROBE/BUSY/ACK handshake timing, why parallel lost to serial (skew, crosstalk, cable cost)
13 Analog interfacing and DAC: why analog, resolution and step size, binary-weighted and R-2R ladder DACs, worked output voltages, DAC0808 with an op-amp, generating ramp, triangle and sine waves from a port, PWM as a cheap DAC
14 ADC: sampling and quantization recap, resolution, step size and quantization error, flash, successive approximation (worked 8-bit SAR trace), dual-slope and sigma-delta in words, conversion time, ADC0804/0808 with SOC/EOC/OE interfaced to 8255, sample-and-hold
15 Keyboard interfacing: switch bounce and debouncing (hardware RC/latch and software delay), matrix keyboard scanning (4x4 worked scan), ghosting and n-key rollover, PC keyboard scan codes (make/break), PS/2 serial protocol, USB HID keyboards, 8279 controller mention
16 LED and 7-segment displays: LED forward voltage and current-limiting resistor worked example, common anode vs common cathode, segment codes table (0-9 hex values), BCD-to-7-segment decoder (7447/7448), multiplexed displays and persistence of vision, LED dot-matrix boards (bus/shop signs)
17 LCD interfacing: how LCDs work (liquid crystal twists light, needs backlight), 16x2 character LCD with HD44780 controller, RS/RW/E pins, 8-bit vs 4-bit mode, init and command codes (01h clear, 38h, 0Ch, 80h/C0h), writing a string, graphic LCDs
18 Display devices and monitors: CRT basics (raster scan, refresh), LCD/LED-backlit, OLED, pixels, resolution, refresh rate, colour depth and frame buffer size worked example, video adapters (MDA/CGA/VGA to GPU), VGA, DVI, HDMI, DisplayPort, touchscreens (resistive vs capacitive)
19 Printers and scanners: impact (dot-matrix, still used for carbon copies) vs non-impact, inkjet (thermal vs piezo), laser printing steps, thermal receipt printers (shop/bKash agent receipts), DPI, PPM, 3D printers in brief, flatbed scanner and CCD/CIS, OCR mention
20 Storage devices: magnetic hard disk (platters, tracks, sectors, cylinders, seek time, rotational latency worked example at 7200 rpm, transfer time), SSD and flash (NAND, pages/blocks, wear levelling), optical CD/DVD/Blu-ray, interfaces IDE/PATA, SATA, NVMe, RAID levels 0/1/5 briefly
21 System buses: bus anatomy (address, data, control), synchronous vs asynchronous, arbitration, ISA (8/16-bit, 8.33 MHz), PCI (32-bit 33 MHz = 133 MB/s, plug and play, bus mastering), AGP, PCI Express (serial lanes x1/x4/x16, per-lane rates by generation), chipsets
22 USB: why USB replaced many ports, host-hub-device tree (up to 127 devices), speeds (1.5/12/480 Mbps, 5/10/20 Gbps), connectors (A, B, micro, Type-C), enumeration and descriptors, transfer types (control, bulk, interrupt, isochronous), device classes, power (5 V, 500 mA/900 mA, USB-PD)
23 SPI and I2C: why small serial buses on boards, SPI (SCLK, MOSI, MISO, SS, full duplex, modes), I2C (SDA/SCL open drain with pull-ups, 7-bit address, start/stop, ACK, 100/400 kHz), worked byte transfer, comparison, UART vs SPI vs I2C, 1-Wire and CAN mention
24 Sensors and actuators: transducers, signal conditioning (amplify, filter, offset), temperature (LM35 10 mV/°C worked with ADC), light (LDR), IR, ultrasonic distance (HC-SR04 time-to-distance worked), relays with transistor and flyback diode, DC motor with H-bridge and PWM, stepper motor step sequence, servo pulse widths, optocouplers for isolation
25 Wrap-up: designing a complete interface: a digital thermometer/irrigation controller walk-through (sensor, ADC, CPU, display, keypad, relay, timer, interrupt), choosing polling/interrupt/DMA and buses, Bluetooth/Wi-Fi modules note, debugging with a multimeter and logic analyser, what comes next (Embedded Systems, OS drivers, IoT), course recap
