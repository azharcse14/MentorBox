# Course: Simulation & Modeling  (level id "deep-cse-6-5", lesson ids "deep-cse-6-5-01".."-25")
Builds on Probability & Statistics (deep-cse-2-6: random variables, distributions, confidence intervals, hypothesis tests), Calculus &
Differential Equations (deep-cse-1-4: rates of change, ODEs), Numerical Methods (deep-cse-3-4: Euler and Runge-Kutta), Data Structures
(deep-cse-2-2: queues, heaps/priority queues) and Algorithms (deep-cse-3-1). Those are only recapped; here the focus is building a model of a
real system, feeding it realistic randomness, running it and trusting (or not trusting) what comes out. Later courses go deeper, so keep these
light and point forward: Operations Research (deep-cse-6-10: linear programming, inventory and queuing as optimisation), Data Science
(deep-cse-6-14), Parallel & High Performance Computing (deep-cse-7-5: big parallel simulations), Reinforcement Learning (deep-cse-8-4:
simulators as training worlds), Robotics (deep-cse-8-5). Banks-Carson-Nelson-Nicol / Law-Kelton style, with small worked tables (LCG
streams, hand simulations, queue formulas, confidence intervals) checked with Python. Tool names (Arena, AnyLogic, SimPy, GPSS, NetLogo,
Vensim) appear only as version-neutral examples.
01 What simulation and modeling are: system, model, simulation in plain words, the flight-simulator idea, why simulate (cost, safety, time compression, what-if questions), when simulation is the right tool and when it is not (analytic answer exists, no data, cost more than benefit), advantages and disadvantages, short history (Buffon's needle, Manhattan Project Monte Carlo, GPSS), course map
02 Systems, components and types of models: system and environment, boundary, entities, attributes, activities, events, state variables, resources, endogenous vs exogenous; physical, mathematical, simulation models; static vs dynamic, deterministic vs stochastic, continuous vs discrete systems and models, classification of a bank, a water tank and a dice game worked
03 Steps in a simulation study: Banks' 12 steps (problem formulation, objectives and project plan, model conceptualisation, data collection, model translation, verification, validation, experimental design, production runs and analysis, more runs, documentation and reporting, implementation), feedback loops between steps, a Dhaka hospital pharmacy queue walked through every step, common project failures
04 Probability review for simulation: experiment, sample space, random variable, discrete vs continuous, PMF, PDF, CDF, expectation and variance worked with a dice and a uniform variable, independence, law of large numbers and why averages of many runs settle, central limit theorem preview for output analysis (recap deep-cse-2-6)
05 Distributions used in simulation: Bernoulli, binomial, geometric, Poisson (worked P(X=k)), uniform, exponential (memoryless worked), normal (68-95-99.7), triangular (expert guesses), Erlang/gamma, Weibull (lifetimes), lognormal, empirical distributions, which distribution models what (arrivals, service, failures, demand)
06 The Poisson process and arrival modeling: counting process, three assumptions, number of arrivals is Poisson, gaps are exponential, worked bus stop and bKash agent examples, merging and splitting streams worked, non-stationary Poisson for rush hours (thinning idea), batch arrivals, when arrivals are not Poisson (scheduled appointments)
07 Random number generation: why we need U(0,1), true vs pseudo-random, properties wanted (uniform, independent, fast, long period, repeatable), midsquare method worked and its failure, linear congruential generator worked (X0=27, a=17, c=43, m=100), full period conditions (Hull-Dobell) worked with m=16, multiplicative LCG, seeds and streams, RANDU warning, modern generators (Mersenne Twister, PCG idea), never use rand() casually for serious work
08 Testing random numbers: hypothesis testing recap, frequency tests (chi-square worked with 100 numbers in 10 classes, Kolmogorov-Smirnov worked with 5 numbers), independence tests (runs up and down worked, autocorrelation idea, gap and poker tests brief), significance level and why passing a test is not proof, what failing tells you
09 Random variate generation I, inverse transform: the idea (invert the CDF), exponential worked (X = -ln(1-R)/lambda), uniform(a,b), triangular, Weibull, discrete distributions by table lookup worked (demand per day), empirical continuous distributions with linear interpolation worked, when the inverse has no closed form
10 Random variate generation II: acceptance-rejection worked with a simple density, efficiency 1/c, Poisson by multiplying uniforms worked, convolution (Erlang as sum of exponentials, binomial as sum of Bernoullis), normal by Box-Muller worked and by sum of 12 uniforms, composition idea, checking generated variates with a histogram
11 Monte Carlo simulation I: what Monte Carlo is, estimating pi with darts worked, hit-or-miss vs sample-mean integration worked for x squared on [0,1], error shrinks like 1/sqrt(n) (4x samples for 2x accuracy worked), Buffon's needle, when Monte Carlo beats formulas (many dimensions)
12 Monte Carlo II, applications and variance reduction: project duration risk with uncertain task times worked, reliability of a system of parts, financial risk idea, spreadsheet Monte Carlo steps, variance reduction (common random numbers, antithetic variates worked, control variates and importance sampling briefly), seeds for reproducibility
13 Discrete-event simulation concepts: event, activity, delay, entity, state, simulation clock, future event list, next-event vs fixed-increment time advance (worked comparison), the event-scheduling loop step by step, world views (event scheduling, process interaction, activity scanning), why DES can simulate a year in seconds
14 Hand simulation of a single-server queue: the table method, generating interarrival and service times from random digits, columns (arrival, service begin, wait, end, idle, time in system) worked for a tea stall with 8 customers, computing average wait, probability of waiting, server utilisation, what the small run can and cannot tell you
15 Building a DES program: future event list as a priority queue (heap, recap deep-cse-2-2), state variables and statistical counters, time-weighted averages (area under Q(t)) worked, M/M/1 simulator pseudo-code step by step, tracing the first events, simulation languages and libraries (GPSS, Arena, SimPy, AnyLogic) version-neutral, process-oriented code with generators
16 Queuing systems fundamentals: calling population (finite vs infinite), arrival process, service mechanism, queue discipline (FIFO, LIFO, SIRO, priority), capacity, Kendall notation A/B/c/N/K/D decoded, customer behaviour (balking, reneging, jockeying), measures L, Lq, W, Wq, rho, Little's law worked and why it is so general, stability rho < 1
17 The M/M/1 queue: birth-death idea, balance equations for the first states, Pn = (1-rho) rho^n, L, Lq, W, Wq formulas worked (lambda 10, mu 12 per hour), why waiting explodes as rho goes to 1 (table at 0.5, 0.8, 0.9, 0.95, 0.99), M/M/1/N finite capacity and blocking worked, checking a simulator against formulas
18 Multi-server and general queues: M/M/c and the Erlang C formula worked (c=2), one shared line vs separate lines compared, M/G/1 and the Pollaczek-Khinchine formula worked, M/D/1 halves the M/M/1 queue, effect of service variability, networks of queues and Jackson's idea, cost models (server cost vs waiting cost) worked
19 Simulation of inventory systems: why stock matters, demand, lead time, ordering cost, holding cost, shortage cost, EOQ recap worked, periodic review (order-up-to) and (s, S) policies, hand simulation table for a pharmacy shop over 10 days worked, newsvendor-style decision with Monte Carlo, comparing policies by total cost (point to deep-cse-6-10)
20 Continuous simulation: state changes smoothly, models as differential equations (recap deep-cse-1-4), Euler method worked (cooling cup of tea), step size and error, Runge-Kutta mention (deep-cse-3-4), water tank, logistic population growth, predator-prey, SIR epidemic model worked for a few steps (COVID-style), combined discrete-continuous models
21 System dynamics and agent-based modeling: stocks, flows, feedback loops (reinforcing and balancing), delays, causal loop diagrams (Dhaka traffic and road building), agent-based modeling (agents, rules, environment, emergence), Schelling segregation and traffic jam examples, cellular automata (Game of Life rules), choosing DES vs SD vs ABM
22 Input modeling: collecting data (and its traps), histograms and bin width, choosing a family, estimating parameters (sample mean for exponential and Poisson worked, normal mean and sd), goodness of fit (chi-square and KS recap applied), Q-Q plot idea, no data (triangular from expert min, mode, max), time-varying and correlated inputs, garbage in garbage out
23 Verification, validation and credibility: verification (did we build the model right) vs validation (did we build the right model), verification techniques (tracing, simple cases, animation, comparing with M/M/1 formulas, code review), validation (face validity, testing assumptions, input-output comparison with real data, Turing test), calibration loop, Naylor-Finger three-step approach, credibility and documentation
24 Output analysis: why one run is not enough, terminating vs non-terminating simulations, independent replications, confidence interval worked with t-distribution, how many replications for a target precision worked, initial transient and warm-up (Welch's method idea), batch means, comparing two designs (paired-t with common random numbers worked), common traps (autocorrelated data treated as independent)
25 Applications and capstone: optimisation via simulation and design of experiments brief, digital twins, applications (manufacturing lines, hospitals, traffic signals, network simulation like ns-3, games and physics, weather and epidemics), capstone: Dhaka metro rail ticket counters (invented numbers) from problem to recommendation, ethics and limits of models, course recap and what next (deep-cse-6-10, deep-cse-6-14, deep-cse-7-5, deep-cse-8-4, deep-cse-8-5)
