# Course: Management Information Systems  (level id "deep-cse-4-7", lesson ids "deep-cse-4-7-01".."-25")
Builds on Database Management Systems (deep-cse-4-2: tables, SQL, transactions, OLTP vs OLAP, warehouses), Computer Networks
and Software Engineering (deep-cse-4-4) and System Analysis & Design (deep-cse-4-6: SDLC, requirements, DFDs), which are only
mentioned here. Focus: how organizations use information systems, seen from a manager's chair: types of IS, business processes,
infrastructure, BI, e-commerce, enterprise systems, decisions, strategy, ethics, security, value and change.
Running examples: a Dhaka garments exporter, a supershop chain, bKash, Daraz, a commercial bank.
01 What MIS is: data vs information vs knowledge (and wisdom), what a system is, information system = people + process + technology + data, IS vs IT, the MIS field and why CSE students study it, course map
02 Information systems in organizations: what an organization is, structure (hierarchy, departments), levels (operational, middle, senior), functional areas (sales/marketing, production, finance/accounting, HR), how IS changes organizations (flatter, faster), IS department roles (CIO, analysts, programmers)
03 Qualities and value of information: accurate, timely, complete, relevant, concise, accessible, verifiable; value of information = better decision minus cost; information overload; GIGO; worked example of a late stock report
04 Business processes: what a process is, inputs/activities/outputs, order-to-cash and procure-to-pay walked through, cross-functional processes, how IS automates and redesigns them, BPR vs continuous improvement, process mapping idea (light; DFDs are in deep-cse-4-6)
05 Types of IS I, TPS: transaction processing systems, batch vs online (real-time) processing, characteristics (high volume, routine, accuracy), POS and ATM examples, TPS as the data source for all other systems
06 Types of IS II, MIS reports and DSS: management information systems (scheduled, exception, on-demand, drill-down reports), decision support systems (model base, data base, user interface, what-if, goal seeking, sensitivity analysis), examples
07 Types of IS III, ESS, KMS, office and collaboration systems: executive support systems and dashboards, knowledge management systems (tacit vs explicit knowledge, knowledge capture/sharing), office automation, collaboration tools, the pyramid summary of all IS types
08 Decision making: structured, semi-structured and unstructured decisions, Simon's intelligence-design-choice(-implementation) model, rational vs bounded rationality, who decides what at which level, group decision support, biases
09 IT infrastructure: hardware, software, networks, data management, services; evolution (mainframe, client/server, internet, cloud and mobile), Moore's law idea, total cost of ownership worked example, build vs buy
10 Cloud computing for business: IaaS/PaaS/SaaS, public/private/hybrid, pay-as-you-go economics, benefits and risks (lock-in, data location, outages), when a small Bangladeshi business should use SaaS
11 Data resource management: why data is an asset, file vs database recap (brief, see deep-cse-4-2), data quality and data governance, master data, data administration vs database administration, a manager's view of a database
12 Business intelligence and analytics: OLTP vs OLAP recap, data warehouse and data mart, ETL, OLAP operations (slice, dice, drill down, roll up), dashboards and KPIs, descriptive/predictive/prescriptive analytics, big data (volume, velocity, variety)
13 Telecommunications and the internet for business: networks and the internet in a manager's view, intranet and extranet, VPN, mobile and wireless, mobile financial services in Bangladesh (bKash, Nagad, Rocket idea), IoT in business brief
14 E-commerce I, concepts: what e-commerce is, unique features (ubiquity, global reach, standards, richness, interactivity, personalization), B2C, B2B, C2C, G2C, m-commerce, social commerce (Facebook pages), Daraz and Chaldal style examples
15 E-commerce II, business models and payments: revenue models (sales, advertising, subscription, transaction fee, affiliate, freemium), marketplace vs inventory model, cash on delivery, payment gateways, logistics and returns, trust
16 Enterprise systems I, ERP: islands of information problem, single central database, ERP modules, best practices and customization trade-off, benefits, implementation risks and costs, an ERP for a garments factory
17 Enterprise systems II, SCM: supply chain (upstream/downstream), bullwhip effect worked example, push vs pull (build-to-stock vs build-to-order), just-in-time, SCM software for planning and execution, RMG buyers and lead time
18 Enterprise systems III, CRM: customer lifetime value, operational vs analytical CRM, sales force automation, customer service, marketing campaigns, churn, a bank or telecom CRM example
19 Competitive strategy I: competitive advantage, Porter's five forces with a Bangladeshi industry worked through, generic strategies (cost leadership, differentiation, focus), using IS for each
20 Competitive strategy II: value chain (primary and support activities), where IS adds value, network effects and platforms, switching costs, why advantage does not last, strategic alignment
21 Ethics, privacy and IP: ethical principles, information rights and privacy, consent, tracking and cookies, intellectual property (copyright, patents, trade secrets, piracy), accountability and liability, digital divide, Bangladesh context
22 Security and controls I, threats: why systems are vulnerable, malware types, phishing and social engineering, hacking, DoS, identity theft, insider threats, mobile wallet fraud examples, business value of security
23 Security and controls II, management framework: risk assessment worked (likelihood x impact), security policy, general vs application controls, authentication (passwords, OTP, biometrics, MFA), firewalls, encryption basics, backups, business continuity and disaster recovery, auditing
24 IS projects and their value: why IS projects fail, project selection, cost-benefit worked (payback, ROI, NPV idea), tangible vs intangible benefits, change management and user resistance, managing risk (light on SDLC, see deep-cse-4-6 and deep-cse-4-4)
25 Digital transformation and wrap-up: what digital transformation means, e-governance and Digital Bangladesh services, AI and automation in business, platforms, careers (business analyst, ERP consultant, IS auditor, data analyst), course recap
